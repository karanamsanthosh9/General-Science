## 1. Definitions of Acids and Bases

### 1.1 Arrhenius Definition

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Acid | Produces H⁺ (H₃O⁺) in aqueous solution | HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻ |
| Base | Produces OH⁻ in aqueous solution | NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻ |

### 1.2 Brønsted-Lowry Definition

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Acid | Proton (H⁺) DONOR | HCl donates H⁺ to water |
| Base | Proton (H⁺) ACCEPTOR | NH₃ accepts H⁺ |

### 1.3 Lewis Definition

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Acid | Electron pair ACCEPTOR | BF₃ accepts electron pair |
| Base | Electron pair DONOR | NH₃ donates electron pair |

## 2. pH Scale

```
pH = -log[H⁺]
pOH = -log[OH⁻]
pH + pOH = 14 (at 25°C)
```

| pH Range | Nature | Example |
|---------|--------|---------|
| 0–6 | Acidic | Lower pH = more acidic |
| 7 | Neutral | Pure water |
| 8–14 | Basic (alkaline) | Higher pH = more basic |

## 7. Neutralisation Reaction

```
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
```

**Applications:**
- Antacid (NaHCO₃ or Mg(OH)₂) neutralises excess stomach acid (HCl)
- Lime (Ca(OH)₂) neutralises acidic soil
- Baking powder: NaHCO₃ + tartaric acid → CO₂ released → batter rises

## 9. Important Reactions — PYQ

```
CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂     (Formation of temporary hard water)
Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂    (Boiling removes temporary hardness)

NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂  (Baking soda + acid → CO₂ gas)

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Heat        (Quick lime + water → slaked lime; exothermic)
Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O      (Lime water + CO₂ → milky = test for CO₂)

2NaHCO₃ (heat) → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂  (Baking soda → washing soda when heated)
```

## 10. Water Hardness

**Causes:** Dissolved calcium and magnesium salts (bicarbonates, sulphates, chlorides)

| Type | Cause | Removal |
|------|-------|---------|
| Temporary hardness | Ca(HCO₃)₂, Mg(HCO₃)₂ (bicarbonates) | Boiling, adding Ca(OH)₂ (Clark's method) |
| Permanent hardness | CaSO₄, MgSO₄, CaCl₂, MgCl₂ | Adding Na₂CO₃ (washing soda), ion exchange |

## 11. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** pH < 7 = acidic; pH > 7 = basic; pH = 7 = neutral. A LOWER pH = MORE acidic (HIGHER H⁺ concentration).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** **Baking soda = NaHCO₃** (sodium BIcarbonate). **Washing soda = Na₂CO₃** (sodium carbonate). Different chemicals with different uses.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** **Milk of magnesia = Mg(OH)₂**, NOT Ca(OH)₂ (that's slaked lime/whitewash).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** **Plaster of Paris ≠ Gypsum.** Gypsum = CaSO₄·2H₂O; PoP = CaSO₄·½H₂O. Gypsum is heated at 150°C to make PoP. PoP + water → sets hard as gypsum.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** **Formic acid** comes from ants (Latin: formicae = ants), NOT from bees (which use a different compound).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** Antacid tablets use **Mg(OH)₂ or NaHCO₃ or Al(OH)₃** — NOT NaOH (caustic soda would burn).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 7:** Bleaching powder releases **Cl₂** in contact with acid → chlorine does the bleaching (not the bleaching powder directly).
