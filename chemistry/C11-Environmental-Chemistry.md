## 1. Atmospheric Composition and Layers

**Atmospheric layers:**

| Layer | Altitude | Key Feature |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Troposphere | 0–12 km | Weather occurs here; temperature decreases with altitude |
| Stratosphere | 12–50 km | Contains ozone layer (20–30 km); temperature increases with altitude |
| Mesosphere | 50–80 km | Coldest layer; meteors burn here; temperature decreases |
| Thermosphere | 80–500 km | Very high temperature; aurora occurs; satellites |
| Exosphere | > 500 km | Outermost; merges with space |

## 2. Air Pollution

### 2.1 Primary Pollutants

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| CO (carbon monoxide) | Incomplete combustion, vehicle exhaust | Binds to haemoglobin (200× stronger than O₂) → oxygen deprivation → death |
| SO₂ (sulphur dioxide) | Coal burning, volcanoes | Acid rain (SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄); respiratory disease |
| NOₓ (nitrogen oxides) | Vehicle exhaust, power plants | Acid rain (NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃); photochemical smog |
| Hydrocarbons (unburned) | Vehicle exhaust | Photochemical smog |
| Particulate matter (PM 2.5, PM 10) | Dust, diesel exhaust | Lung disease, cardiovascular problems |
| Lead (Pb) | Old leaded petrol (now banned in most countries) | Neurotoxin; damages brain especially in children |
| H₂S | Industrial, natural | Foul smell (rotten egg); toxic |

### 2.2 Secondary Pollutants

| Pollutant | Formation | Effect |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| Ozone (ground-level O₃) | NOₓ + Hydrocarbons + Sunlight → O₃ | Photochemical smog; lung irritant; plant damage |
| PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate) | Similar to O₃ formation | Eye irritant; photochemical smog |
| H₂SO₄ (sulphuric acid mist) | SO₂ + O₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ | Acid rain |

## 3. Acid Rain

**Definition:** Rainfall with pH < 5.6 due to dissolved acids.

**Formation:**
```
Sulphur path:  S + O₂ → SO₂; 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃; SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄
Nitrogen path: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO; 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂; 3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO
```

**Effects of Acid Rain:**

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Damages aquatic ecosystems | Acidifies lakes, kills fish |
| Damages forests | Leaches nutrients, weakens trees |
| Corrodes monuments | Dissolves limestone, marble (Taj Mahal damaged by SO₂) |
| Damages metal structures | Bridges, statues corrode faster |
| Soil acidification | Kills soil microbes, reduces fertility |

**Taj Mahal and acid rain:** Marble (CaCO₃) reacts with H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ (gypsum) — causes "Marble cancer" (surface becomes powdery).

## 4. Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming

### 4.1 Greenhouse Effect (Natural)

- Short-wavelength solar radiation passes through atmosphere → absorbed by Earth's surface
- Earth re-emits long-wavelength infrared radiation
- Greenhouse gases (CO₂, H₂O vapour, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs) absorb and re-emit this IR
- **Natural greenhouse effect is essential** — without it, Earth's average temperature would be −18°C instead of +15°C

### 4.2 Enhanced Greenhouse Effect (Problem)

- Increased CO₂, CH₄, N₂O from human activities → more heat trapped → global warming

**Greenhouse gases and their potency:**

| Gas | GWP (100-year, vs CO₂=1) | Main Source |
|-----|--------------------------|------------|
| CO₂ | 1 | Fossil fuels, deforestation |
| CH₄ | 25 | Livestock, rice paddies, landfills, natural gas |
| N₂O | 298 | Fertilisers, agriculture, burning |
| HFCs | 1,000–22,000 | Refrigerants (CFC replacements) |
| PFCs | 7,000–12,000 | Aluminium smelting, semiconductors |
| SF₆ | 22,800 | Electrical equipment insulation |
| CFCs | 4,000–14,000 | Old refrigerants (also deplete ozone) |

## 5. Ozone Layer and Depletion

### 5.1 Ozone Layer

- Located in the **stratosphere (15–35 km altitude)**
- Ozone (O₃) absorbs harmful **UV-B and UV-C radiation** from the sun
- Without ozone layer: increased UV → skin cancer, cataracts, immune suppression, crop damage

### 5.2 Ozone Depletion

**Cause:** CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) — freons used as refrigerants, aerosol propellants

**Mechanism:**
```
CFCl₃ + UV → •CFCl₂ + Cl•
Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂     (one Cl atom can destroy 100,000 O₃ molecules!)
ClO• + O• → Cl• + O₂
```

**Ozone hole:** Largest over Antarctica due to polar vortex, ice crystal reactions

**Montreal Protocol (1987):** International treaty to phase out ozone-depleting substances (CFCs, HCFCs). Most successful international environmental agreement.

## 6. Water Pollution

### 6.1 Types and Sources

| Pollutant Type | Sources | Effects |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| Organic matter | Sewage, agricultural runoff | Increases BOD, depletes O₂, kills fish |
| Heavy metals | Industrial discharge | Minamata disease (Hg), Itai-Itai (Cd), brain damage (Pb) |
| Pesticides | Agricultural runoff | Bioaccumulation, endocrine disruption |
| Nitrates/Phosphates | Fertiliser runoff | Eutrophication, algal bloom |
| Thermal pollution | Power plant cooling water | Decreases dissolved O₂, kills aquatic life |
| Oil spills | Shipping accidents | Kills marine life, coats birds |
| Plastics/microplastics | Disposal | Marine ecosystem damage |

### 6.2 BOD (Biological Oxygen Demand)

- **Definition:** Amount of dissolved O₂ consumed by biological organisms decomposing organic matter in water at given temperature
- **Clean water:** BOD < 1 mg/L
- **Polluted water:** BOD > 5 mg/L (indicates high organic waste)
- **Heavily polluted:** BOD > 10 mg/L

### 6.3 Eutrophication

```
Excess N & P (fertiliser runoff) → algal bloom → algae die → bacteria decompose algae
→ bacteria consume all O₂ → fish and other organisms die (hypoxia/anoxia)
```

## 8. Famous Environmental Disasters

| Disaster | Year | Cause | Effect |
|---------|------|-------|--------|
| Minamata disease | 1956, Japan | Mercury (methylmercury) in water from Chisso factory | Neurological damage, birth defects |
| Itai-Itai disease | 1912+, Japan | Cadmium pollution from Mitsui Mining | Bone softening, kidney damage |
| Bhopal gas tragedy | 1984, India | MIC (methyl isocyanate) leak from Union Carbide | 2,000+ immediate deaths; thousands more affected |
| Chernobyl | 1986, Ukraine | Nuclear reactor explosion | Massive radioactive contamination |
| London smog | 1952 | SO₂ + smoke + fog | 12,000+ deaths |

> **Bhopal gas tragedy:** MIC = **methyl isocyanate (CH₃NCO)** — reacted with water to form lethal gases.
