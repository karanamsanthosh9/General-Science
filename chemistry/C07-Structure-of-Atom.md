## 1. Subatomic Particles

| Particle | Symbol | Charge | Mass | Location | Discovery |
|---------|--------|--------|------|---------|-----------|
| **Proton** | p⁺ | +1 | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 1 amu | Nucleus | Rutherford (1919) |
| **Neutron** | n⁰ | 0 | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 1 amu | Nucleus | Chadwick (1932) |
| **Electron** | e⁻ | −1 | 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg = 1/1836 amu | Orbitals/shells | Thomson (1897) |

```
Atomic number (Z) = number of protons
Mass number (A)   = protons + neutrons
Neutrons          = A − Z
```

## 2. Atomic Models — Historical Development

| Model | Scientist | Year | Key Idea | Limitation |
|-------|-----------|------|---------|-----------|
| Solid sphere | John Dalton | 1803 | Atom is indivisible, solid sphere | Couldn't explain electrical properties |
| Plum pudding | J.J. Thomson | 1904 | Electrons embedded in positive sphere (like plums in pudding) | Rutherford's experiment disproved it |
| Nuclear model | Rutherford | 1911 | Nucleus + electrons orbiting (like solar system); most of atom is empty space | Couldn't explain stability (accelerating electrons should lose energy and spiral in) |
| Planetary model | Niels Bohr | 1913 | Electrons orbit in fixed energy levels (shells); no energy lost in fixed orbits | Only worked for hydrogen; failed for multi-electron atoms |
| Quantum mechanical model | Schrödinger/Heisenberg | 1926 | Orbitals as probability regions | Current accepted model |

### 2.1 Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment

**Experiment:** Bombarded gold foil with alpha particles; observed deflection on ZnS screen.

**Observations:**
- Most α-particles passed straight through → atom mostly empty space
- Few deflected at small angles → some positive charge inside
- Very few (1 in 20,000) bounced back → very small, dense, positive nucleus

**Conclusions:**
- Atom has a tiny, dense, positively charged **nucleus**
- Most of the atom is **empty space**
- Electrons revolve around the nucleus at a large distance

### 2.2 Bohr's Model (PYQ Favourite)

**Postulates:**
1. Electrons revolve in fixed circular orbits called **energy levels (shells)**
2. Each shell has a fixed energy — electrons do NOT lose energy while in orbit
3. Electrons can jump between shells by absorbing/emitting energy: ΔE = hf
4. Maximum electrons in shell n = **2n²**

**Shell Capacity:**

| Shell | Name | Max Electrons |
|-------|------|--------------|
| 1 | K | 2 (= 2×1²) |
| 2 | L | 8 (= 2×2²) |
| 3 | M | 18 (= 2×3²) |
| 4 | N | 32 (= 2×4²) |

## 3. Electronic Configuration

**Rules:**
1. **Aufbau principle:** Fill orbitals in order of increasing energy
2. **Pauli exclusion principle:** Each orbital holds max 2 electrons with opposite spins
3. **Hund's rule:** Fill all orbitals of same energy with 1 electron each before pairing

**Electronic Configuration of First 20 Elements:**

## 4. Isotopes, Isobars, Isotones

| Term | Same | Different | Example |
|------|------|-----------|---------|
| **Isotopes** | Atomic number Z (same element) | Mass number A (different neutrons) | ¹H, ²H (D), ³H (T); ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C; ²³⁵U, ²³⁸U |
| **Isobars** | Mass number A | Atomic number Z | ¹⁴C (Z=6) and ¹⁴N (Z=7); ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca |
| **Isotones** | Number of neutrons | Atomic number and mass number | ¹⁴C (6p, 8n) and ¹⁵N (7p, 8n) |
| **Isoelectronic** | Number of electrons | Protons/atoms/charge | Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, Ne, F⁻ (all 10 electrons) |

**Important Isotopes — PYQ Table:**

| Element | Common Isotopes | Use |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Hydrogen | ¹H (protium, common), ²H (deuterium D, heavy hydrogen), ³H (tritium, radioactive) | Heavy water (D₂O) as nuclear reactor moderator; tritium in H-bombs |
| Carbon | ¹²C (most abundant, stable), ¹⁴C (radioactive, T½=5730 yr) | C-12 is standard for atomic mass; C-14 for radiocarbon dating |
| Uranium | ²³⁵U (fissile, 0.7% natural), ²³⁸U (fertile, 99.3% natural) | U-235 in nuclear reactors and bombs; U-238 in breeder reactors |
| Iodine | ¹²⁷I (stable), ¹³¹I (radioactive, T½=8 days) | I-131 for thyroid cancer diagnosis and treatment |
| Cobalt | ⁵⁹Co (stable), ⁶⁰Co (radioactive) | Co-60 in cancer radiotherapy (gamma knife) |

## 5. Quantum Numbers (Brief)

| Quantum Number | Symbol | Describes | Values |
|---------------|--------|----------|--------|
| Principal | n | Shell/energy level | 1, 2, 3, 4 ... |
| Azimuthal (angular momentum) | l | Subshell/orbital shape | 0 to n−1 |
| Magnetic | mₗ | Orbital orientation | −l to +l |
| Spin | mₛ | Electron spin | +½ or −½ |

**Subshell names:** l=0 → s; l=1 → p; l=2 → d; l=3 → f

**Orbital filling order:**
```
1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p ...
(use Aufbau diagram / (n+l) rule)
```

## 7. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** **Rutherford discovered the nucleus, NOT the proton.** Proton was discovered by Rutherford in 1919, but the nucleus was established by his 1911 gold foil experiment.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** **Chadwick discovered the NEUTRON (1932)** — a relatively late discovery. This completed our picture of the atom.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** **Isotopes have same Z (same element) but different A** (different neutrons). They have SAME chemical properties but different physical properties (mass, radioactivity).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** **Max electrons = 2n²** per shell. Shell 1 = 2, Shell 2 = 8, Shell 3 = 18, Shell 4 = 32.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** **Bohr's model only worked for hydrogen** (one-electron atoms) — it failed for multi-electron atoms.
