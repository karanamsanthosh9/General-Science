## 1. Polymers — Introduction

**Polymer:** Large molecule made of repeating units (monomers) linked by covalent bonds.

```
n (monomer) → [−monomer−]ₙ (polymer)
```

---

## 2. Classification of Polymers

### 2.1 Natural vs Synthetic

| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| Natural polymers | Natural rubber, cellulose, starch, proteins, DNA, wool, silk |
| Synthetic polymers | Polyethylene, PVC, Nylon, Teflon, Bakelite, polystyrene |

### 2.2 Thermoplastic vs Thermosetting

| Type | Behaviour on heating | Structure | Examples |
|------|---------------------|-----------|---------|
| **Thermoplastic** | Soften on heating, can be reshaped | Linear or branched chains | Polyethylene, PVC, polystyrene, nylon |
| **Thermosetting** | Harden permanently on heating (cannot be reshaped) | Cross-linked 3D network | Bakelite, urea-formaldehyde, melamine |

### 2.3 Addition vs Condensation Polymers

| Type | Mechanism | By-product | Examples |
|------|----------|-----------|---------|
| Addition | Monomers add without losing atoms | None | Polyethylene, PVC, Teflon, polystyrene |
| Condensation | Monomers join with loss of small molecule (H₂O, HCl) | H₂O or HCl | Nylon, Dacron (PET), Bakelite, proteins |

## 4. Fibres — Natural vs Synthetic

### 4.1 Natural Fibres

| Fibre | Source | Composition | Properties | Uses |
|-------|--------|------------|-----------|------|
| **Cotton** | Cotton plant (Gossypium) seed hairs | Cellulose | Soft, absorbent, comfortable | Clothing, thread |
| **Silk** | Silkworm (Bombyx mori) cocoon | Protein (fibroin) | Lustrous, smooth, strong | Luxury clothing, sarees |
| **Wool** | Sheep (Merino most valued) | Protein (keratin) | Warm, elastic, fire-resistant | Winter clothing, carpets |
| **Jute** | Jute plant (Corchorus) stem | Cellulose + lignin | Rough, biodegradable | Sacks, mats, rope |
| **Flax (Linen)** | Flax plant (Linum usitatissimum) stem | Cellulose | Cool, smooth | Bed linen, clothing |

### 4.2 Synthetic Fibres

| Fibre | Polymer | Properties | Uses |
|-------|---------|-----------|------|
| **Nylon** | Polyamide (Nylon-6,6) | Strong, elastic, quick-drying | Stockings, ropes, toothbrushes |
| **Polyester (Dacron/Terylene)** | Polyethylene terephthalate (PET) | Wrinkle-resistant, durable | Clothing, bottles |
| **Acrylic (Orlon)** | Polyacrylonitrile | Wool-like, warm, lightweight | Blankets, sweaters |
| **Rayon (viscose)** | Regenerated cellulose (semi-synthetic) | Silk-like, absorbent | Clothing, carpets (hybrid natural-synthetic) |
| **Spandex (Lycra/Elastane)** | Polyurethane | Very elastic (stretches 500%+) | Sportswear, swimwear |

## 5. Rubber

### 5.1 Natural Rubber

- Source: **Latex** from *Hevea brasiliensis* (rubber tree)
- Chemical: **Polyisoprene** (cis-1,4-polyisoprene)
- Properties: Elastic, waterproof, but sticky when hot, brittle when cold

### 5.2 Vulcanisation (Charles Goodyear, 1839)

- **Process:** Heating natural rubber with **sulphur (1–3%)**
- **Result:** Sulphur forms cross-links between polymer chains
- **Effect:** Harder, less sticky, more elastic, wider temperature range, more durable

> **Goodyear tyres** named after Charles Goodyear (inventor of vulcanisation).

## 6. Plastics — Environmental Issues

| Problem | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Non-biodegradable | Most plastics last hundreds of years in environment |
| Microplastics | Plastics break into tiny particles, contaminate water/food chains |
| Burning hazard | PVC releases HCl; polystyrene releases carcinogens |
| Marine pollution | Single-use plastics kill marine life |

## 7. Ceramics and Composites

| Material | Description | Examples |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Ceramics | Non-metallic, inorganic, crystalline | Bricks, pottery, porcelain, glass |
| Glass | Amorphous (non-crystalline) solid; SiO₂-based | Window glass, Pyrex, fibre optic |
| Composites | Two or more materials combined | Fibreglass (glass fibre + resin), carbon fibre, concrete (cement + aggregate) |
