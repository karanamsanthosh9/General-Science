## 1. Nervous System Overview

```
Nervous System
├── Central Nervous System (CNS)
│   ├── Brain
│   └── Spinal Cord
└── Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)
    ├── Somatic (voluntary) — skeletal muscles
    └── Autonomic (involuntary)
        ├── Sympathetic (fight-or-flight)
        └── Parasympathetic (rest-and-digest)
```

## 2. Neuron (Nerve Cell)

| Part | Function |
|------|---------|
| **Cell body (Soma)** | Contains nucleus and organelles |
| **Dendrites** | Receive signals from other neurons; short, branched |
| **Axon** | Transmits signal away from cell body; can be very long (up to 1 m) |
| **Myelin sheath** | Insulates axon; speeds signal transmission; made by Schwann cells |
| **Nodes of Ranvier** | Gaps in myelin sheath; saltatory conduction |
| **Synapse** | Junction between two neurons; uses neurotransmitters |

**Types of neurons:**
- **Sensory (afferent):** Carry signals from body/senses TO brain/spinal cord
- **Motor (efferent):** Carry signals FROM brain/spinal cord TO muscles/glands
- **Interneurons:** Connect sensory and motor neurons; within CNS only

**Reflex arc:**
```
Stimulus → Sensory receptor → Sensory neuron → Spinal cord (interneuron)
→ Motor neuron → Effector (muscle/gland) → Response
```
Reflex does NOT need brain — spinal cord is sufficient.

| Region | Location | Functions | Key Facts | PYQ |
|--------|---------|---------|----------|-----|

## 4. Endocrine System

**Endocrine glands** secrete **hormones** directly into blood (no ducts → "ductless glands").

**Hormone properties:**
- Chemical messengers
- Produced in small quantities
- Carried in blood
- Act on specific target organs/cells (through receptor binding)
- Can be proteins (insulin), steroids (cortisol, sex hormones), or amino acid derivatives (thyroxine, adrenaline)

---

## 5. Endocrine Glands — Complete PYQ Master Table

| Gland | Location | Hormone(s) | Function | Disorder | PYQ |
|-------|---------|-----------|---------|---------|-----|
| **Hypothalamus** | Below thalamus in brain | Releasing hormones (GnRH, TRH, CRH, GHRH) and inhibiting hormones; ADH, Oxytocin | Controls pituitary; regulates body temperature, hunger, thirst, sleep | — | 🔥 |
| **Pituitary gland** ("Master gland") | Base of brain (sella turcica) | **Anterior:** GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, Prolactin; **Posterior:** ADH, Oxytocin | Controls ALL other endocrine glands; growth | GH excess (child) = Gigantism; GH excess (adult) = Acromegaly; GH deficiency = Dwarfism | 🔥 |
| **Thyroid gland** | Neck (in front of larynx) | Thyroxine (T4), Triiodothyronine (T3), Calcitonin | Regulates metabolism, growth, development; Ca²⁺ balance | Hypothyroid = Goitre (Iodine deficiency), Myxoedema (adult), Cretinism (infant); Hyperthyroid = Graves' disease | 🔥 |
| **Parathyroid glands** (4 tiny glands) | Behind thyroid | PTH (Parathyroid hormone) | Increases blood Ca²⁺ (mobilises from bones, increases absorption); opposes calcitonin | Hypo = Hypocalcaemia, tetany; Hyper = Hypercalcaemia, osteoporosis | ⭐ |
| **Adrenal glands** (on top of kidneys) | Cortex: Cortisol, Aldosterone, Androgens; Medulla: **Adrenaline (Epinephrine)**, Noradrenaline | Cortex: stress response, salt/water balance, anti-inflammatory; Medulla: fight-or-flight | Cortex: Addison's disease (hypo), Cushing's syndrome (hyper); Medulla: tumour (phaeochromocytoma) | 🔥 |
| **Pancreas** (Islets of Langerhans) | Abdomen (behind stomach) | **Insulin** (β cells) → lowers blood glucose; **Glucagon** (α cells) → raises blood glucose; Somatostatin (δ cells) | Blood glucose regulation | **Diabetes mellitus** (Type 1 = no insulin; Type 2 = insulin resistance); Hypoglycaemia (too much insulin) | 🔥 |
| **Gonads — Ovaries** | Pelvis (female) | **Estrogen** (follicles), **Progesterone** (corpus luteum) | Female secondary sex characteristics; menstrual cycle; pregnancy | Premature ovarian failure; PCOS | ⭐ |
| **Gonads — Testes** | Scrotum (male) | **Testosterone** (Leydig cells) | Male secondary sex characteristics; spermatogenesis | Hypogonadism | ⭐ |
| **Thymus** | Chest (mediastinum) | Thymosin | T-lymphocyte maturation; immunity; large in children, atrophies in adults | — | ⭐ |
| **Pineal gland** | Brain (below corpus callosum) | **Melatonin** | Regulates sleep-wake cycle (circadian rhythm); responds to darkness | Jet lag; seasonal affective disorder (SAD) | ⭐ |

## 6. Hormone Functions — Quick Reference

| Hormone | Source | Target | Effect |
|---------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Insulin** | Pancreas (β cells) | Liver, muscle, fat | **Lowers** blood glucose; stores as glycogen/fat |
| **Glucagon** | Pancreas (α cells) | Liver | **Raises** blood glucose; breaks down glycogen → glucose |
| **Adrenaline** | Adrenal medulla | Heart, lungs, liver, muscles | Fight-or-flight: ↑heart rate, ↑blood pressure, ↑blood glucose, ↑breathing rate |
| **Thyroxine** | Thyroid | All cells | ↑Metabolic rate; growth; development |
| **ADH (Vasopressin)** | Posterior pituitary (hypothalamus) | Kidneys | Reduces urine output (water retention); regulates blood pressure |
| **Aldosterone** | Adrenal cortex | Kidneys | Retains Na⁺ → retains water → ↑blood pressure |
| **Cortisol** | Adrenal cortex | Widespread | Stress response; anti-inflammatory; ↑blood glucose (gluconeogenesis) |
| **GH (Growth Hormone)** | Anterior pituitary | Bones, muscles | Promotes growth; protein synthesis |
| **FSH** | Anterior pituitary | Ovaries/Testes | Stimulates follicle/sperm production |
| **LH** | Anterior pituitary | Ovaries/Testes | Triggers ovulation; testosterone production |
| **Oxytocin** | Posterior pituitary | Uterus, breast | Uterine contractions (labour); milk ejection (breastfeeding) |
| **Prolactin** | Anterior pituitary | Breast | Milk PRODUCTION (not ejection) |
| **Melatonin** | Pineal gland | Brain | Sleep; circadian rhythm |
| **Testosterone** | Testes | Multiple | Male sex characteristics; spermatogenesis; anabolic |
| **Estrogen** | Ovaries | Multiple | Female sex characteristics; endometrial growth |
| **Progesterone** | Corpus luteum/Placenta | Uterus | Maintains pregnancy; endometrial maintenance |

## 7. Diabetes Mellitus

| Feature | Type 1 | Type 2 |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Cause | Autoimmune destruction of β cells → NO insulin | Insulin resistance (cells don't respond to insulin) + eventual β cell decline |
| Age of onset | Usually childhood/young | Usually middle-age/older (but now also young) |
| Body weight | Usually normal | Often overweight/obese |
| Treatment | **Must use insulin** | Diet + exercise + oral medication; sometimes insulin |
| Genetics | Strong HLA association | Polygenic + lifestyle |

**Diagnosis:**
- Fasting blood glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL
- Random glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5%

## 8. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** **Pituitary is the "master gland"** BUT it is CONTROLLED BY the **hypothalamus** (the real master behind the master).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** **Insulin LOWERS blood glucose; Glucagon RAISES it.** Both from pancreas but opposite effects.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** **Adrenaline** comes from **adrenal MEDULLA** (inner part), NOT adrenal cortex. Cortisol comes from cortex.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** **Goitre** can result from both iodine deficiency (hypothyroidism) AND iodine excess — the gland enlarges in both cases.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** **Cerebellum = balance and coordination**, NOT intelligence or thinking. Cerebrum = thinking.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** **Medulla oblongata** controls involuntary vital functions (breathing, heartbeat) — damage here is fatal.
