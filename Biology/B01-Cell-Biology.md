## 2. Cell — Introduction

**Cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow):**
1. All living organisms are made of cells
2. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells

**Types of cells:**
- **Prokaryotic:** No membrane-bound nucleus; simpler; small (1–10 μm)
- **Eukaryotic:** Membrane-bound nucleus; complex; larger (10–100 μm)

---

## 3. Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic Cell | Eukaryotic Cell |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Nucleus** | ABSENT (no nuclear membrane) | PRESENT (membrane-bound) |
| **Membrane-bound organelles** | Absent | Present |
| **DNA** | Circular, in cytoplasm (nucleoid region) | Linear, in nucleus |
| **Histones** | Absent | Present |
| **Cell size** | 1–10 μm (smaller) | 10–100 μm (larger) |
| **Ribosome** | 70S | 80S (but 70S in mitochondria and chloroplasts) |
| **Cell wall** | Present (peptidoglycan in bacteria) | Present only in plants (cellulose), fungi (chitin) |
| **Examples** | Bacteria, Archaea, Cyanobacteria (Blue-green algae) | All animals, plants, fungi, protists |
| **Flagella** | Simple (non-membranous) | Complex (membranous) |

## 5. Mitochondria — Deep Dive (PYQ Favourite)

**Why mitochondria are called "powerhouse":**
- Produce **ATP (adenosine triphosphate)** — the universal energy currency of cells
- Via **cellular respiration:** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + **38 ATP**
- Inner membrane is folded into **cristae** — increases surface area for ATP synthesis

**Special properties of mitochondria:**
- Have their **own DNA** (circular, like bacteria) and **70S ribosomes**
- Can self-replicate (semi-autonomous)
- Evidence for **endosymbiotic theory** (mitochondria evolved from ancient bacteria engulfed by cells)
- **Maternally inherited** (mitochondria come from egg cell, not sperm)

## 7. Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane)

**Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer and Nicolson, 1972):**
- Phospholipid bilayer — **hydrophilic heads** face outward; **hydrophobic tails** face inward
- Proteins embedded in the lipid bilayer (integral proteins) or on surface (peripheral proteins)
- Membrane is **fluid** — components can move laterally
- **Cholesterol** stabilises membrane fluidity

**Transport across membrane:**

| Type | Energy Required | Direction | Examples |
|------|----------------|-----------|---------|
| Passive diffusion | No (passive) | High → Low concentration | O₂, CO₂ crossing membrane |
| Osmosis | No (passive) | High → Low water potential | Water movement in/out of cells |
| Facilitated diffusion | No (passive) | High → Low (via channel/carrier) | Glucose, amino acids |
| Active transport | Yes (ATP) | Low → High (against gradient) | Na⁺/K⁺ pump |
| Endocytosis | Yes | Into cell | Phagocytosis, pinocytosis |
| Exocytosis | Yes | Out of cell | Secretion of hormones, enzymes |

## 8. Cell Division

### 8.1 Mitosis

**Purpose:** Growth, repair, and replacement of somatic (body) cells; asexual reproduction

**Location:** All actively dividing body cells (except reproductive cells)

**Phases:** IPMAT — **Interphase → Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase**

**Outcome:**
```
1 diploid (2n) parent cell → 2 identical diploid (2n) daughter cells
Number of chromosomes: same as parent
DNA: replicated during interphase (S phase)
```

**Key events:**
- **Prophase:** Chromosomes condense; spindle forms; nuclear envelope breaks down
- **Metaphase:** Chromosomes align at cell equator (metaphase plate) — **best stage to count/observe chromosomes**
- **Anaphase:** Sister chromatids separate; pulled to opposite poles
- **Telophase:** Nuclear envelopes re-form; chromosomes decondense → 2 nuclei

### 8.2 Meiosis

**Purpose:** Formation of gametes (sperm, egg, pollen) for sexual reproduction

**Location:** Gonads (testes, ovaries in animals); anthers, ovaries in plants

**Consists of:** Meiosis I + Meiosis II

**Outcome:**
```
1 diploid (2n) parent cell → 4 haploid (n) daughter cells
Chromosome number: HALVED
Genetic diversity: due to crossing over (prophase I) and independent assortment
```

**Key features:**
- **Crossing over** in Prophase I: exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes → genetic recombination
- **Independent assortment:** Homologous chromosomes separate independently → many possible combinations

### 8.3 Mitosis vs Meiosis — Comparison

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Purpose | Growth, repair | Gamete production |
| Where | Somatic cells | Gonads |
| Divisions | 1 | 2 (Meiosis I + II) |
| Daughter cells | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosome number | Same (diploid) | Halved (haploid) |
| Genetic variation | No (identical) | Yes (crossing over) |
| Crossing over | No | Yes (Prophase I) |
| Occurs in | Most body cells | Reproductive cells only |

## 11. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** **Ribosome is NOT membrane-bound** — it is present in ALL living cells including prokaryotes. It is the ONLY organelle found in prokaryotes.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** **Mitochondria and Chloroplasts have their own DNA and 70S ribosomes** (like bacteria). This is endosymbiotic theory evidence.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** **Centrosome is ABSENT in mature plant cells** — plants use other mechanisms for spindle formation.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** **Cell wall is NOT the same as cell membrane.** Cell wall = rigid outer layer; Cell membrane = selectively permeable inner layer. ANIMALS have NO cell wall.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** **Mature RBC has NO nucleus** (in mammals) — nucleus is lost during development. Fish and amphibian RBCs retain nucleus.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** **Mitosis produces 2 identical cells; Meiosis produces 4 genetically diverse haploid cells.**
