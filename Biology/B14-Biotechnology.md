# B14 — Biotechnology 📖 Priority 4

> **Exam Importance:** Low frequency in most competitive exams. Tested more in APPSC AEE and SSC. Key topics: recombinant DNA technology, PCR, GMO, biofuel, transgenic organisms, vaccines from biotechnology.

---

## 1. Biotechnology — Definition

**Biotechnology:** Use of living organisms or their components to develop useful products, processes, or services.

**Traditional biotechnology:** Fermentation (bread, beer, cheese, curd)
**Modern biotechnology:** Genetic engineering, recombinant DNA technology, GMOs

---

## 2. Recombinant DNA Technology (Genetic Engineering)

**Steps:**
1. **Isolate** gene of interest from donor organism
2. **Cut** gene using restriction endonucleases (molecular scissors)
3. **Insert** gene into vector (plasmid, phage, virus)
4. **Introduce** recombinant vector into host cell (transformation)
5. **Select** transformed cells
6. **Express** the gene → desired protein

**Key tools:**

| Tool | Function |
|------|---------|
| **Restriction enzymes** | Cut DNA at specific sequences (restriction sites); "molecular scissors" |
| **DNA ligase** | Joins DNA fragments; "molecular glue" |
| **Plasmid** | Circular DNA of bacteria; used as vector (carrier) |
| **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)** | Amplifies specific DNA sequences millions of times; invented by Kary Mullis (1983) |
| **Gel electrophoresis** | Separates DNA fragments by size |
| **DNA probes** | Detect specific sequences |

## 3. Applications of Biotechnology

### 3.1 Medicine

| Application | Example | Details |
|------------|---------|---------|
| **Insulin production** | Human insulin (*Humulin*) | First recombinant DNA product for medicine (1982); produced in *E. coli* or yeast |
| **Vaccines** | Hepatitis B vaccine | Recombinant yeast-produced surface antigen |
| **Growth hormone** | rHGH | Produced in bacteria |
| **Erythropoietin (EPO)** | For anaemia | Produced in CHO cells |
| **Monoclonal antibodies** | Diagnostics, cancer therapy | Hybridoma technology |
| **Gene therapy** | Treating genetic diseases | Introduce normal gene to replace defective one |
| **CRISPR-Cas9** | Gene editing | Precise genome modification; Nobel Prize 2020 (Charpentier & Doudna) |

### 3.2 Agriculture — GMOs (Genetically Modified Organisms)

| Crop | Modification | Benefit |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Bt cotton (Bollgard)** | Bt toxin gene (*Bacillus thuringiensis*) | Resistance to bollworm (insect pest) |
| **Bt brinjal** | Same Bt toxin | Insect resistance |
| **Golden rice** | β-carotene biosynthesis genes | Source of Vitamin A (to combat deficiency) |
| **Herbicide-resistant crops** | Modified to tolerate herbicide application | Allows herbicide use to kill weeds |
| **Flavr Savr tomato** | Delayed ripening gene | Extended shelf life; first approved GMO food |

### 3.3 Environment

| Application | Details |
|------------|---------|
| **Bioremediation** | Microbes clean up oil spills, heavy metals |
| **Biofuel** | Ethanol from corn/sugarcane; biodiesel from algae |
| **Biosensors** | Detect pollutants |
| **Phytoremediation** | Plants absorb heavy metals from soil |

## 4. PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)

**Inventor:** Kary Mullis (1983) — Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1993

**Process:**
```
1. Denaturation: Heat to ~95°C → DNA double strand separates
2. Annealing: Cool to ~55°C → Primers bind to target sequences
3. Extension: Heat to ~72°C → DNA polymerase (Taq polymerase) extends primers
→ Repeat 30–40 cycles → 2³⁰–⁴⁰ copies of target DNA
```

**Uses:**
- DNA fingerprinting (forensics)
- Diagnosing genetic diseases
- Pathogen detection (COVID-19 RT-PCR)
- Paternity testing
- Ancient DNA analysis

---

## 5. DNA Fingerprinting

- **Inventor:** Alec Jeffreys (1984, UK)
- Based on VNTR (Variable Number Tandem Repeats) — unique for each individual
- **Applications:** Forensics (crime investigation), paternity testing, identifying missing persons, evolutionary studies

---

## 6. Cloning

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Molecular cloning | Cloning genes/DNA sequences | Recombinant DNA in bacteria |
| Reproductive cloning | Create genetically identical organism | Dolly the sheep (first mammal cloned, 1996, Ian Wilmut) |
| Therapeutic cloning | Produce stem cells for medical use | Potential for growing replacement organs |

**Dolly the sheep:**
- First mammal cloned from adult somatic cell
- Technique: Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer (SCNT)
- Born 1996; died 2003 (age 6, premature ageing)
