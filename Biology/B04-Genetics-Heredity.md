## 1. DNA and Heredity

### 1.1 DNA — Structure

**Double Helix Model:** Discovered by **James Watson and Francis Crick (1953)** using X-ray crystallography data from **Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins**.

```
DNA = Deoxyribonucleic Acid
Components: 
- Deoxyribose sugar (5-carbon)
- Phosphate group
- Nitrogenous base
```

**Base Pairing Rules (Chargaff's Rules):**
```
Purines (double ring): Adenine (A) and Guanine (G)
Pyrimidines (single ring): Thymine (T) and Cytosine (C)

A pairs with T (2 hydrogen bonds)
G pairs with C (3 hydrogen bonds)
Note: RNA uses Uracil (U) instead of Thymine (T)
```

| DNA | RNA |
|-----|-----|
| Deoxyribose sugar | Ribose sugar |
| Thymine (T) | Uracil (U) |
| Double-stranded | Usually single-stranded |
| Found in nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts | Found in nucleus, cytoplasm, ribosomes |
| Stores genetic information | Carries and processes genetic information |

## 2. Key Genetic Terms

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Gene** | Segment of DNA coding for a specific protein/trait | Gene for eye colour |
| **Allele** | Alternative forms of a gene | Brown (B) or blue (b) allele for eye colour |
| **Dominant** | Allele expressed when present (even one copy) | B (brown) masks b |
| **Recessive** | Allele expressed only when homozygous | bb = blue eyes |
| **Homozygous** | Two identical alleles | BB or bb |
| **Heterozygous** | Two different alleles | Bb |
| **Genotype** | Genetic makeup (allele combination) | Bb |
| **Phenotype** | Observable physical trait | Brown eyes |
| **Locus** | Specific position of gene on chromosome | — |
| **Gamete** | Sex cell (haploid) — egg or sperm | 23 chromosomes |
| **Diploid (2n)** | Two sets of chromosomes | All body cells = 46 |
| **Haploid (n)** | One set of chromosomes | Gametes = 23 |
| **Codominance** | Both alleles expressed equally | Blood type AB (both A and B expressed) |
| **Incomplete dominance** | Neither fully dominant; blend | Red × White → Pink flowers |

## 3. Mendel's Laws

### 3.1 Gregor Mendel — Father of Genetics

- Conducted experiments on **pea plants (Pisum sativum)** from 1856–1863
- Chose 7 traits: seed shape, seed colour, pod colour, pod shape, flower position, plant height, flower colour

### 3.2 Law of Segregation (First Law)

> *"Each organism has two alleles for each trait. During gamete formation, these two alleles segregate so that each gamete carries only one allele."*

**Monohybrid cross example:**
```
P:  Tall (TT) × Short (tt)
F1: All Tall (Tt) — dominant trait appears
F2: 3 Tall (TT + 2Tt) : 1 Short (tt)
    Genotypic ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
    Phenotypic ratio: 3:1
```

### 3.3 Law of Independent Assortment (Second Law)

> *"Alleles of different genes are distributed independently into gametes."*

**Dihybrid cross:**
```
F2 phenotypic ratio: 9:3:3:1
```

## 5. Sex Determination

```
Female: XX  (2 X chromosomes)
Male: XY    (1 X + 1 Y chromosome)

Eggs: all carry X (XX → X + X; both X)
Sperm: 50% carry X, 50% carry Y

Sex of child determined by FATHER'S sperm (X or Y)
```

**Karyotype of sex chromosomes:**
- XX = female (homogametic sex)
- XY = male (heterogametic sex)

> **Exam critical:** Sex of the baby is determined by the FATHER, not the mother — the mother always provides X.

## 6. Sex-Linked (X-Linked) Diseases

**Definition:** Genes located on X chromosome; males (XY) are more commonly affected because they have only ONE X chromosome.

| Disease | Inheritance | Who affected | Key Fact |
|---------|------------|-------------|---------|
| **Haemophilia A** | X-linked recessive | Males primarily | Inability to clot blood; Queen Victoria was carrier; "Royal disease" |
| **Haemophilia B** (Christmas disease) | X-linked recessive | Males primarily | Same symptoms as A; different clotting factor (Factor IX) |
| **Red-green colour blindness** | X-linked recessive | Males (~8%) >> Females (~0.5%) | Cannot distinguish red from green |
| **Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD)** | X-linked recessive | Males only (females = carriers) | Progressive muscle weakening; dystrophin gene absent |
| **Turner syndrome** | 45, XO | Females only | Short stature; no secondary sex characteristics; infertile |
| **Klinefelter syndrome** | 47, XXY | Males | Infertile; gynecomastia; tall |

**Carrier female:**
- X^H X^h → carrier (has one normal X and one haemophilia X; appears normal)
- Sons of carrier: 50% chance of haemophilia

## 8. Single-Gene Disorders

| Disorder | Inheritance | Cause | Key Feature |
|---------|------------|-------|-------------|
| **Sickle cell anaemia** | Autosomal recessive | Mutation in HBB gene → abnormal Hb-S | RBCs become sickle-shaped; pain crises; provides some malaria resistance (heterozygote advantage) |
| **Cystic fibrosis** | Autosomal recessive | CFTR gene mutation | Thick mucus in lungs/pancreas; breathing problems |
| **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** | Autosomal recessive | Phenylalanine hydroxylase enzyme absent | Cannot metabolise phenylalanine → brain damage |
| **Albinism** | Autosomal recessive | Melanin production absent (tyrosinase deficiency) | White skin, hair; pink eyes; photosensitive |
| **Huntington's disease** | Autosomal dominant | HTT gene expansion (CAG repeat) | Progressive neurodegeneration; onset 30–50 years |
| **Marfan syndrome** | Autosomal dominant | FBN1 (fibrillin) gene mutation | Tall, long limbs; aortic problems |

## 9. DNA Replication, Transcription, Translation

```
DNA → (Replication) → DNA     [nucleus; semi-conservative]
DNA → (Transcription) → mRNA  [nucleus; template strand of DNA]
mRNA → (Translation) → Protein [ribosome; codon = 3 nucleotides = 1 amino acid]
```

**Genetic code:**
- **Codon:** 3 nucleotide sequence on mRNA → codes for one amino acid
- **Anticodon:** Complementary sequence on tRNA
- 64 possible codons (4³ = 64); 20 amino acids → code is degenerate
- **Start codon:** AUG (also codes for Methionine)
- **Stop codons:** UAA, UAG, UGA

## 10. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** **Sex is determined by the FATHER** (sperm carries X or Y). The mother always contributes X.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** **Haemophilia = inability to CLOT blood** (bleeding disorder) — NOT excess clotting.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** **Down syndrome = 47 chromosomes** (trisomy 21). It is NOT caused by sex chromosomes.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** **Colour blindness** is more common in MALES (X-linked recessive; males have only one X, so one copy of recessive gene expresses).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** **Watson and Crick** described the structure of DNA (1953) using Franklin's X-ray data. Mendel discovered principles of heredity (1860s).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** **Sickle cell anaemia** heterozygotes (carriers) have some protection against malaria — this is why the allele is maintained in malaria-endemic areas.
