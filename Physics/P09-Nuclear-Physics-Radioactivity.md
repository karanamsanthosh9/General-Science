## 2. Structure of the Nucleus

- **Nucleus** contains protons (+) and neutrons (0) → called **nucleons**
- **Atomic number (Z)** = number of protons
- **Mass number (A)** = protons + neutrons
- **Neutrons** = A − Z

**Nuclear Terms:**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Isotopes | Same Z, different A (same element) | ¹H, ²H (deuterium), ³H (tritium) |
| Isobars | Same A, different Z | ¹⁴C and ¹⁴N |
| Isotones | Same number of neutrons, different Z | ¹⁴C (6p,8n) and ¹⁵N (7p,8n) |
| Isoelectronic | Same number of electrons | N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺ |

## 3. Radioactivity

**Definition:** Spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable nuclei.

**Discovery:** Henri Becquerel (1896) discovered radioactivity in uranium. Marie Curie coined the term "radioactivity" and discovered Polonium and Radium.

## 4. Types of Radiation — Master Comparison Table

| Property | Alpha (α) | Beta (β) | Gamma (γ) |
|---------|-----------|---------|----------|
| Nature | Helium nucleus (2 protons + 2 neutrons) | High-speed electron | Electromagnetic wave (high-energy photon) |
| Symbol | ₂⁴He | e⁻ or β⁻ | γ |
| Charge | +2 | −1 | 0 |
| Mass | 4 amu | ~1/1836 amu | 0 (massless) |
| Speed | Slowest (~1/20 c) | Fast (~0.99c) | Speed of light (c) |
| Penetrating power | Lowest (stopped by paper/skin) | Medium (stopped by 3mm Al sheet) | Highest (needs thick lead/concrete) |
| Ionising power | Highest | Moderate | Lowest |
| Range in air | A few cm | Few metres | Very large |
| Deflected by electric/magnetic field | Yes (toward −ve plate) | Yes (toward +ve plate) | No |

> **Memory:** "Alpha is Armoured (high ionisation) but short-range; Gamma Goes through everything"
> **Penetration order:** α < β < γ

## 5. Nuclear Reactions

### 5.1 Alpha Decay

```
²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + ⁴He (α particle)
₉₂        ₉₀       ₂
(mass number decreases by 4; atomic number by 2)
```

### 5.2 Beta Decay

```
¹⁴C → ¹⁴N + e⁻ (β particle) + antineutrino
₆       ₇
(mass number unchanged; atomic number increases by 1)
```

### 5.3 Gamma Decay

- Nucleus transitions from excited state to ground state
- No change in A or Z — only energy released as γ photon

## 6. Fission vs Fusion — Master Comparison

| Property | Nuclear Fission | Nuclear Fusion |
|---------|----------------|---------------|
| Process | Heavy nucleus SPLITS into smaller nuclei | Light nuclei COMBINE to form heavier nucleus |
| Example | U-235 + n → Ba + Kr + 3n + energy | H + H → He + energy (sun) |
| Energy released | Very large | Even larger (per unit mass) |
| Required condition | Slow neutrons (critical mass needed) | Extremely high temperature (10⁷ – 10⁸ K) |
| Weapon | Atom bomb (uncontrolled fission) | Hydrogen bomb (uncontrolled fusion) |
| Controlled use | Nuclear reactor | Experimental (still being developed) |
| Waste | Radioactive waste (problem) | Less radioactive waste (He produced) |
| Starting material | U-235, Pu-239 | Deuterium (²H), Tritium (³H) |
| Source of sun's energy | No | Yes (H → He fusion) |
| Chain reaction | Yes | No (needs constant high T) |

> **Key Fact:** Fusion releases MORE energy per unit mass than fission — that's why H-bomb is more powerful than atom bomb.

## 7. Nuclear Reactor

### 7.1 Principle
Controlled nuclear fission of U-235 to produce heat → heat generates steam → steam drives turbine → electricity produced.

### 7.2 Components of a Nuclear Reactor

| Component | Material | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Fuel** | Uranium-235 (U-235) or Plutonium-239 (Pu-239) | Fissile material — undergoes fission |
| **Moderator** | Heavy water (D₂O), Graphite, Beryllium | Slows down fast neutrons to thermal neutrons (needed for U-235 fission) |
| **Control rods** | Boron (B) or Cadmium (Cd) | Absorbs excess neutrons to control chain reaction rate; inserted/withdrawn to control power |
| **Coolant** | Heavy water, CO₂, liquid sodium, regular water | Removes heat from reactor core to generate steam |
| **Reflector** | Graphite or beryllium | Reflects escaping neutrons back into core |
| **Shielding** | Lead and concrete | Absorbs radiation to protect workers and environment |

### 7.3 Chain Reaction

```
¹n + ²³⁵U → ²³⁶U → Ba + Kr + 3¹n + energy
                              ↓
               Each neutron can split another U-235
               → Chain reaction
```

- **Critical mass:** Minimum mass of fissile material needed to sustain chain reaction
- **Sub-critical:** Chain reaction dies out
- **Super-critical:** Uncontrolled reaction (atom bomb!)
- **Critical:** Sustained controlled reaction (nuclear reactor)

## 8. Half-Life

> **Definition:** Time required for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay.

```
N(t) = N₀ × (1/2)^(t/T½)
```
- N₀ = initial number of nuclei
- T½ = half-life
- t = time elapsed

**Example:**
- If T½ = 10 years and we start with 1000 atoms:
  - After 10 years: 500 atoms
  - After 20 years: 250 atoms
  - After 30 years: 125 atoms
  - After n half-lives: 1000 × (1/2)ⁿ atoms

**Half-life values — PYQ Facts:**

| Isotope | Half-life | Application |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Carbon-14 | 5730 years | Radiocarbon dating (archaeology) |
| Uranium-238 | 4.5 × 10⁹ years | Geological dating |
| Iodine-131 | 8 days | Thyroid cancer treatment |
| Radon-222 | 3.8 days | — |

## 9. Radiocarbon Dating

- **Principle:** Living organisms maintain equilibrium of C-12 and C-14 (from atmosphere). After death, C-14 decays with T½ = 5730 years; C-12 remains stable.
- **Method:** Measure ratio of C-14/C-12 in dead organic material → calculate age
- **Range:** Up to ~50,000 years
- **Limitation:** Only for organic (carbon-containing) materials

## 12. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** Fission ≠ Fusion. **Fission** = splitting heavy atoms (U-235). **Fusion** = joining light atoms (H). These are commonly confused.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** **Fusion releases MORE energy per unit mass** than fission — H-bomb more powerful than atom bomb.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** **Moderator slows neutrons** (does NOT absorb them all). **Control rods absorb neutrons** (regulate rate).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** **Penetrating power:** α < β < γ. **Ionising power:** α > β > γ (inverse relationship).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** **Alpha particles** = helium nuclei (+2 charge). NOT just "protons".

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** **Carbon dating uses C-14**, not C-12. C-12 is stable; C-14 is radioactive.
