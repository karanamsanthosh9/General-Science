## 1. Simple Machines — Introduction

**Simple machine:** A device that makes work easier by changing the direction or magnitude of force.

**Key Terms:**
- **Load (W):** Resistance force overcome by the machine
- **Effort (E):** Force applied to operate the machine
- **Mechanical Advantage (MA):** MA = Load/Effort = W/E
- **Velocity Ratio (VR):** VR = Velocity of effort / Velocity of load = distance effort moves / distance load moves
- **Efficiency (η):** η = MA/VR × 100% = (useful output / input) × 100%
- Ideal machine: MA = VR; η = 100% (no friction)

```
MA = W/E
VR = d_effort / d_load
η = (MA/VR) × 100% = Output work / Input work × 100%
```

## 2. Types of Simple Machines

### 2.1 Lever

**Definition:** A rigid rod that can rotate about a fixed point called **fulcrum (F)**.

**Principle:** W × load arm = E × effort arm (principle of moments)

```
W × l₁ = E × l₂
MA = l₂ / l₁
```

**Classes of Levers:**

| Class | Fulcrum Position | MA | Example |
|-------|-----------------|-----|---------|
| **First class** | Between load and effort | Can be > 1, = 1, or < 1 | See-saw, scissors, pliers, crowbar, balance scale |
| **Second class** | Load is between fulcrum and effort | Always > 1 (effort arm > load arm) | Wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener, door (hinge = fulcrum) |
| **Third class** | Effort is between fulcrum and load | Always < 1 (speed gain, not force gain) | Tongs, tweezers, fishing rod, human forearm/jaw |

> **Memory:** "**FLE, FLE, FLE**" for 1st, 2nd, 3rd class — Fulcrum-Load-Effort positions:
> 1st: F between L and E
> 2nd: L between F and E
> 3rd: E between F and L

### 2.2 Pulley

| Type | Description | MA | VR | Example |
|------|-------------|----|----|---------|
| Single fixed pulley | Attached to ceiling; just changes direction | 1 | 1 | Well/flag pole |
| Single movable pulley | Attached to load | 2 | 2 | — |
| Block and tackle | Multiple pulleys | n (number of rope segments) | n | Cranes, elevators |

### 2.3 Wheel and Axle

- Large wheel attached to smaller axle
- Effort applied on wheel; load on axle
- MA = radius of wheel / radius of axle
- Examples: Steering wheel, screwdriver, doorknob, water pump handle, windlass

### 2.4 Inclined Plane

- A flat surface raised at one end
- MA = L/h = length/height
- Examples: Ramps, stairs, wedge, screw

### 2.5 Screw

- Converts rotary motion to linear motion
- High mechanical advantage
- Examples: Vice, screw jack, jackscrew (lift heavy loads)

### 2.6 Wedge

- Two inclined planes joined back-to-back
- Converts applied force to splitting force at right angles
- Examples: Knife, axe, chisel, needle, nail, pin

## 3. Human Body as a Lever System

| Body Part | Class | Fulcrum | Load | Effort |
|-----------|-------|---------|------|--------|
| Head nodding (neck) | 1st class | Atlas vertebra | Head weight | Neck muscles |
| Standing on tiptoe | 2nd class | Toes | Body weight | Calf muscles |
| Forearm lifting | 3rd class | Elbow joint | Load in hand | Bicep muscle |
| Jaw (biting) | 3rd class | Jaw joint (TMJ) | Food | Jaw muscles |

---

## 4. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** 2nd class levers always have MA > 1 (wheelbarrow: easier to push heavy load).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** 3rd class levers have MA < 1 — they give SPEED advantage, not force advantage (forearm, tweezers).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** A single fixed pulley has MA = 1 — it only changes direction of force, does NOT reduce effort.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** Efficiency is always ≤ 100% due to friction. Ideal machine is a theoretical concept.
