## 1. Photoelectric Effect

### 1.1 Definition
Emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of sufficient frequency falls on it.

**Key Terms:**
- **Threshold frequency (ν₀):** Minimum frequency of light required to eject electrons
- **Work function (φ):** Minimum energy needed to remove an electron from metal surface
- **Stopping potential (V₀):** Voltage needed to stop the emitted electrons

### 1.2 Einstein's Explanation (1905)

```
Energy of photon: E = hν
KE of emitted electron: KE = hν − φ = h(ν − ν₀)
where h = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s
```

**Key Facts:**
- Einstein received Nobel Prize (1921) for explaining the photoelectric effect (NOT for relativity)
- Proved wave-particle duality of light
- Number of electrons emitted depends on **intensity** of light
- KE of electrons depends on **frequency**, NOT intensity
- Below threshold frequency: NO electrons emitted, regardless of intensity

### 1.3 Applications

| Application | Use |
|------------|-----|
| Solar cells (photovoltaic) | Convert light to electricity |
| Photomultiplier tubes | Light detection |
| Digital cameras | CCD sensors |
| Automatic doors | Light beam interrupted |

## 2. X-Rays

### 2.1 Discovery
X-rays were discovered by **Wilhelm Röntgen in 1895** → Nobel Prize in Physics (1901, the first ever Nobel in Physics).

### 2.2 Properties of X-Rays

| Property | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Nature | Electromagnetic radiation |
| Wavelength | 0.01 nm – 10 nm (very short) |
| Frequency | 3 × 10¹⁶ – 3 × 10¹⁹ Hz |
| Speed | 3 × 10⁸ m/s (speed of light) |
| Charge | Zero (neutral) |
| Penetrating power | Very high (passes through soft tissue) |
| Ionising | Yes (harmful in excess) |
| Detected by | Photographic film, fluorescent screen |

### 2.3 Production of X-Rays

- Produced when **fast-moving electrons** hit a metal target (tungsten)
- High voltage (kV) accelerates electrons
- Two types: **Bremsstrahlung** (braking radiation) and **characteristic X-rays**

### 2.4 Applications of X-Rays

| Application | Details |
|------------|---------|
| Medical radiography | Chest X-ray, bone imaging |
| CT scan | Cross-sectional body images |
| Dental X-ray | Tooth decay detection |
| Airport security | Scanning luggage |
| Crystal structure determination | X-ray crystallography (Bragg's law) |
| Industrial inspection | Finding cracks in metals |

## 3. de Broglie Hypothesis (Wave-Particle Duality)

> *"All matter has wave-like properties — the de Broglie wavelength of a particle:*"

```
λ = h/mv = h/p
```
- h = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s
- m = mass of particle
- v = velocity of particle
- p = momentum

**Key Points:**
- Larger mass → smaller wavelength → wave nature undetectable (e.g., cricket ball)
- Smaller mass → larger wavelength → wave nature important (e.g., electron)
- Confirmed by electron diffraction experiments

## 4. Planck's Quantum Theory

```
E = nhν    (energy is quantised)
E = hν     (energy of one photon)
h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s (Planck's constant)
```

- Energy is not continuous but comes in discrete packets called **quanta** (photons for light)
- Explains blackbody radiation spectrum

---

## 5. Compton Effect

- **X-ray photon collides with electron** → scattered X-ray has longer wavelength (lower energy)
- Proved particle nature of X-rays (photons)
- A. H. Compton (1923) — Nobel Prize 1927

---

## 6. Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle

```
Δx × Δp ≥ h/4π
```
- Cannot simultaneously measure position AND momentum of a particle with perfect precision
- More precisely position is known → less precisely momentum is known → and vice versa
