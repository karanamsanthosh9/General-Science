## 2. Nature of Sound

| Property | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Nature of wave | Longitudinal (compressions and rarefactions) |
| Requires medium? | YES — cannot travel in vacuum |
| Speed in vacuum | Zero (no propagation) |
| Speed in air (0°C) | ≈ 332 m/s |
| Speed in air (25°C) | ≈ 346 m/s |
| Speed increases with | Temperature: v ∝ √T |
| Speed: Solid vs Gas | Solid > Liquid > Gas (fastest in solids) |

**Wave Equation:**
```
v = fλ
f = v/λ
λ = v/f
```
- v = wave speed (m/s)
- f = frequency (Hz)
- λ = wavelength (m)

## 3. Classification by Frequency

| Type | Frequency Range | Examples |
|------|----------------|---------|
| **Infrasonic** | < 20 Hz | Earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, elephants (15 Hz), whales, weather systems |
| **Audible (Sonic)** | 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz (20 kHz) | Human hearing range |
| **Ultrasonic** | > 20,000 Hz (20 kHz) | Bats (40–100 kHz), dolphins, dogs, SONAR, medical imaging |

> **Human hearing range:** 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (decreases with age)
> **Bats use echolocation** — emit ultrasonic pulses and detect echoes to navigate and hunt

## 4. Speed of Sound in Different Media

| Medium | Approximate Speed | Relative |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| Air (0°C) | 332 m/s | Slowest |
| Air (25°C) | 346 m/s | — |
| Water | 1500 m/s | ~4.5× faster than air |
| Steel | 5100–5960 m/s | Fastest (solid) |
| Wood | 3000–4000 m/s | Solid |
| Rubber | ~60 m/s | Very slow |

> **Key fact:** Speed of sound in solid > liquid > gas
> **Speed of light >> speed of sound** — that's why we see lightning before we hear thunder

**Effect of temperature on speed of sound:**
```
v = 332 + 0.6t   m/s     (approximate formula for air)
where t = temperature in °C
```

## 6. Echo and Reverberation

### 6.1 Echo

- **Condition for echo:** Distance from reflecting surface must be > **17 m** (in air, at room temperature)
- **Why 17 m?** Human ear can distinguish two sounds separated by at least 0.1 s; sound travels 332 × 0.1 ≈ 33.2 m total (17 m one way)
```
Minimum distance for echo = (332 × 0.1)/2 = 16.6 ≈ 17 m
```

### 6.2 Reverberation

- **Definition:** Persistence/prolonging of sound in an enclosed space due to multiple reflections
- **Difference from echo:** Echo = distinct repetition; Reverberation = prolonged sound (merges with original)
- **Problem:** Concert halls — too much reverberation muddles sound
- **Solution:** Curtains, carpets, acoustic tiles to absorb sound

### 6.3 Applications of Echoes

| Application | Principle | Use |
|------------|---------|-----|
| SONAR | Ultrasound echo timing | Detect submarines, measure ocean depth, find fish |
| Echolocation | Echo of ultrasound | Bats, dolphins for navigation |
| Medical ultrasound | Echo of ultrasound | Pregnancy imaging, organ scanning |
| Distance measurement | Echo timing | Range finding |

## 7. SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging)

```
SONAR = Sound Navigation And Ranging
```

- Uses **ultrasound pulses** (not audible sound)
- Emits pulse → receives echo → calculates distance
```
d = v × t/2    (v = speed in water, t = time for echo)
```
- **Applications:** Submarine detection, ocean floor mapping, fish finding, underwater navigation

## 9. Doppler Effect

> *"The apparent change in frequency (pitch) of sound due to relative motion between the source and the observer."*

| Scenario | Apparent Frequency | Sound |
|---------|------------------|-------|
| Source approaching observer | Higher than actual | Higher pitch (like ambulance siren getting closer) |
| Source moving away | Lower than actual | Lower pitch (siren fading away) |
| Observer approaching source | Higher | Higher pitch |
| Observer moving away | Lower | Lower pitch |

**Applications:**
- Radar guns (traffic speed measurement) — uses Doppler effect
- Weather radar — Doppler radar for wind speed
- Medical Doppler ultrasound — blood flow measurement
- Astronomical: redshift of stars moving away from us

## 11. Acoustics of Buildings

**Good acoustics requirements:**
- Adequate loudness (sufficient intensity)
- No echoes (reflected sound should be controlled)
- Controlled reverberation (0.5–1.5 s optimum for music halls)
- No noise from outside (sound insulation)
- No focusing effect from curved surfaces

**Sound absorbing materials:** Carpet, curtains, upholstered seats, acoustic tiles, fibreboard

## 12. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** Sound CANNOT travel in vacuum — it is NOT an electromagnetic wave and requires a material medium.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** Speed of sound: solid > liquid > gas — sound is FASTEST in solids, NOT in gases.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** Echo ≠ reverberation — echo is distinct (needs > 17 m from reflector); reverberation is prolonged sound.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** Pitch depends on FREQUENCY; Loudness depends on AMPLITUDE — these are frequently swapped.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** Light is a transverse wave; Sound is a LONGITUDINAL wave.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** Speed of sound increases with temperature — hotter air = faster sound.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 7:** Bats use ULTRASOUND (>20 kHz), NOT infrasound, for echolocation.
