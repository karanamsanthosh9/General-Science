## 1. Metals vs Non-Metals — Comparison

| Property | Metals | Non-Metals |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Physical state at room temp | Solid (except Hg — liquid) | Solid (most), liquid (Br), gas (H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂, F₂, noble gases) |
| Lustre | Shiny/lustrous | Dull (except iodine which is lustrous) |
| Electrical conductivity | Good conductors | Poor conductors (except graphite) |
| Thermal conductivity | Good conductors | Poor (except diamond which is excellent) |
| Malleability | Malleable (can be beaten into sheets) | Brittle (if solid) |
| Ductility | Ductile (can be drawn into wires) | Not ductile |
| Hardness | Hard (except alkali metals Na, K etc.) | Variable |
| Melting/Boiling point | Generally high (except Hg, Cs, Ga) | Variable (low to high) |
| Density | Generally high | Generally low |
| Reaction with O₂ | Forms basic/amphoteric oxides | Forms acidic oxides |
| Reaction with water | Reacts (Group 1 violently) | No general reaction |
| Reaction with acids | Displaces H₂ (if above H in reactivity series) | No general reaction |
| Reaction with Cl₂ | Forms ionic chlorides | Forms covalent chlorides |
| Electrons | Lose electrons (form cations, act as reducing agents) | Gain electrons (form anions, act as oxidising agents) |

## 2. Reactivity Series of Metals (Activity Series)

**Order (most to least reactive):**
```
K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Ni > Sn > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au > Pt
```

**Memory:** "**K**ing **Na**poleon **Ca**n **Mg**nify **Al**l **Zn**c **Fe**lony **Ni**ghtly; **Sn**ake **Pb**itter-**H**eads **Cu**rl **Hg**old **Ag**ents **Au**gmented **P**ure **T**o Perfection" 

**Simpler mnemonic:**
> "**Ka Na Chalte Magar Aadam Zindagi Fek Nahi Sakta Paar Hum Chale Hain Aage**"
> K-Na-Ca-Mg-Al-Zn-Fe-Ni-Sn-Pb-H-Cu-Hg-Ag-Au-Pt

**Applications of Reactivity Series:**

| Application | Principle |
|------------|---------|
| Displacement reactions | More reactive metal displaces less reactive from its salt solution |
| Extraction method | More reactive = harder to extract (electrolysis needed); less reactive = easy (heat alone) |
| Rusting/corrosion | More reactive metals corrode faster |
| Galvanisation | Zinc (more reactive) protects iron by sacrificial corrosion |

## 4. Alloys — Complete PYQ Table

> **Alloy** = Homogeneous mixture of two or more metals (or metal + non-metal)

| Alloy | Composition | Properties | Uses | PYQ |
|-------|------------|-----------|------|-----|
| **Steel** | Fe + C (0.3–1.5%) | Hard, strong | Construction, tools, machinery | 🔥 |
| **Stainless steel** | Fe + Cr (10–20%) + Ni (8–10%) | Rust-resistant, hard | Utensils, surgery, architecture, cutlery | 🔥 |
| **Cast iron** | Fe + C (2–4.5%) | Brittle, hard | Pipes, stoves, machine parts, cookware | ⭐ |
| **Wrought iron** | Fe + C (<0.1%) | Soft, malleable, tough | Ornamental work, chains, anchors | 📘 |
| **Pig iron** | Fe + C (3.5–4.5%) + impurities | Brittle | Intermediate product in steel making | 📘 |
| **Brass** | Cu (60–90%) + Zn (10–40%) | Golden, corrosion-resistant | Decorative items, musical instruments, taps, bolts | 🔥 |
| **Bronze** | Cu (80–90%) + Sn (10–20%) | Hard, brownish | Coins, medals, statues, bells, bearings | 🔥 |
| **German silver** (Nickel silver) | Cu + Zn + Ni (no actual silver!) | Silver-like appearance | Silverware, coins, jewellery, musical instruments | ⭐ |
| **Gun metal** | Cu + Sn + Zn | Hard, corrosion-resistant | Guns, bearings, valves, marine hardware | 📘 |
| **Bell metal** | Cu (80%) + Sn (20%) | Sonorous | Bells, gongs | 📘 |
| **Solder** | Pb (50–70%) + Sn (30–50%) | Low melting point | Electrical soldering, plumbing | 🔥 |
| **Amalgam** | Hg + other metals (Ag, Sn, Cu, Zn) | Soft initially, hardens | Dental fillings (being phased out) | ⭐ |
| **Duralumin** | Al (94%) + Cu (4%) + Mg (1%) + Mn (1%) | Light, strong, corrosion-resistant | Aircraft, automobile parts, ship building | 🔥 |
| **Magnalium** | Al + Mg | Light, strong | Aircraft parts, scientific instruments | 📘 |
| **Invar** | Fe (64%) + Ni (36%) | Very low thermal expansion | Precision instruments, clocks, tape measures | 📘 |
| **Nichrome** | Ni + Cr (+ Fe) | High resistance, high melting point | Heating elements (electric heater, toaster) | ⭐ |
| **Type metal** | Pb + Sn + Sb | Expands on solidifying | Printing type | 📘 |
| **Rose's metal** | Pb + Sn + Bi | Very low MP (~94°C) | Safety plugs, fuses, solder | 📘 |

## 7. Extraction of Metals

| Method | Used For | Why |
|--------|---------|-----|
| Electrolysis | Very reactive metals (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) | Cannot be reduced by carbon |
| Reduction by carbon (coke) | Moderately reactive metals (Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb) | More economical |
| Reduction by hydrogen | Less reactive metals (Ni, Cu) | When carbon gives impure product |
| Displacement by more reactive metal | Less reactive (Cu, Ag) | Thermite reaction (Al + Fe₂O₃) |
| Heat alone (no reducing agent) | Least reactive metals (Hg, Ag, Au) | Their oxides decompose on heating |

## 8. Corrosion

- **Rusting of iron:** 4Fe + 3O₂ + 6H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃ → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust)
- Requires both **water and oxygen** (air)
- Salty water accelerates rusting (electrolyte)
- **Prevention of rusting:**
  - Galvanisation (zinc coating)
  - Painting/varnishing
  - Electroplating (Cr, Ni)
  - Alloying (stainless steel)
  - Sacrificial protection (zinc/magnesium blocks on ships)
  - Cathodic protection

## 9. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** **Graphite (carbon) is a non-metal that conducts electricity** — it is an exception to "non-metals don't conduct."

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** **German silver contains NO silver** — it is Cu + Zn + Ni. The name is misleading.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** **Brass = Cu + Zn; Bronze = Cu + Sn.** Commonly confused. Remember: "BraSS has a Zinc in it" — B-r-a-S-S (two S = zinc added).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** **Pure gold (24 carat) is too soft** for jewellery. 22 carat = 22 parts gold + 2 parts Cu/Ag. Lower carat = more alloy metal.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** **Galvanising** = coating iron with **zinc** (NOT tin). Tin plating = different process.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** Rusting requires both **water AND oxygen** — dry air or oxygen-free water alone will NOT cause rusting.
