## 1. States of Matter

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas | Plasma |
|---------|-------|--------|-----|--------|
| Shape | Definite | No definite | No definite | — |
| Volume | Definite | Definite | No definite | — |
| Compressibility | Very low | Low | High | — |
| Fluidity | No | Yes | Yes | — |
| Inter-particle space | Very small | Small | Very large | — |
| Inter-particle forces | Very strong | Strong | Very weak | — |
| Thermal energy vs IMF | IMF dominates | Balanced | Thermal dominates | — |
| Example | Ice, stone | Water, mercury | Steam, air | Sun, lightning |

> **4th state of matter = Plasma** — ionised high-energy gas; found in sun, stars, lightning, fluorescent lamps, neon signs.

## 2. Changes of State

```
Solid ⇌ Liquid ⇌ Gas

Solid → Liquid: Melting (fusion) — absorbs latent heat of fusion
Liquid → Solid: Freezing (solidification) — releases latent heat of fusion
Liquid → Gas: Vaporisation/Boiling — absorbs latent heat of vaporisation
Gas → Liquid: Condensation — releases latent heat of vaporisation
Solid → Gas: Sublimation (directly without liquid phase)
Gas → Solid: Deposition (reverse sublimation)
```

**Sublimation examples:**
- Dry ice (solid CO₂) → CO₂ gas
- Iodine (solid) → iodine vapour
- Camphor → vapour
- Naphthalene (mothballs) → vapour
- Ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl)

## 3. Gas Laws

### 3.1 Boyle's Law (1662)

> *"At constant temperature, the volume of a given mass of gas is inversely proportional to pressure."*

```
PV = constant   (T = constant)
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
P ∝ 1/V   (at constant T)
```

**Application:** Syringe, deep-sea diving, breathing mechanism

### 3.2 Charles' Law (1787)

> *"At constant pressure, the volume of a given mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature."*

```
V/T = constant   (P = constant)
V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂
V ∝ T   (at constant P)
```

**Note:** Temperature MUST be in KELVIN (not Celsius)

**Application:** Hot air balloon (heated air expands, density decreases, balloon rises)

### 3.3 Gay-Lussac's Law (Pressure-Temperature Law)

> *"At constant volume, the pressure of a given mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature."*

```
P/T = constant   (V = constant)
P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂
P ∝ T   (at constant V)
```

**Application:** Pressure in sealed tyre increases in summer; pressure cooker

### 3.4 Combined Gas Law

```
PV/T = constant
P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂
```

### 3.5 Avogadro's Law (1811)

> *"Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of molecules."*

```
V ∝ n   (at constant T and P)
```

- **1 mole of any gas at STP** occupies **22.4 litres (molar volume)**
- **Avogadro's number (Nₐ) = 6.022 × 10²³** molecules/mol

### 3.6 Ideal Gas Equation

```
PV = nRT

where:
P = pressure (Pa or atm)
V = volume (m³ or L)
n = number of moles
R = universal gas constant = 8.314 J/mol·K = 0.082 L·atm/mol·K
T = temperature (Kelvin)
```

## 4. STP and NTP Conditions

| Condition | Temperature | Pressure | Molar Volume |
|---------|------------|---------|-------------|
| **STP** (Standard Temperature and Pressure) | 273.15 K (0°C) | 1 bar (= 10⁵ Pa) | ~22.7 L/mol |
| **Old STP** | 273.15 K (0°C) | 1 atm (101,325 Pa) | 22.4 L/mol |
| **NTP** (Normal Temperature and Pressure) | 293 K (20°C) | 1 atm | 24 L/mol |

> **Exam Note:** Most competitive exams use **1 atm and 0°C** with molar volume = **22.4 L/mol**

## 5. Kinetic Molecular Theory of Gases

**Postulates:**
1. Gas molecules are in constant random motion
2. Volume of molecules is negligible compared to container volume
3. No intermolecular forces (ideal gas)
4. All collisions are perfectly elastic (kinetic energy conserved)
5. Average kinetic energy is directly proportional to absolute temperature

```
KE_avg = (3/2)kT   (k = Boltzmann constant = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K)
```

**Real gases vs Ideal gases:**
- Real gases deviate from ideal at **high pressure and low temperature** (intermolecular forces become significant; molecular volume non-negligible)
- Modified by **van der Waals equation**

## 6. Vapour Pressure and Humidity

- **Vapour pressure:** Pressure exerted by vapour in equilibrium with its liquid
- **Boiling point:** Temperature at which vapour pressure = atmospheric pressure
- **Relative humidity:** Actual vapour pressure / Saturation vapour pressure × 100%

## 7. Diffusion and Effusion (Graham's Law)

```
Rate of diffusion ∝ 1/√(Molar mass)
r₁/r₂ = √(M₂/M₁)
```

- Lighter gases diffuse faster
- H₂ is the fastest diffusing gas (lightest)
- **Application:** Separation of isotopes (U-235 from U-238 using UF₆), smell spreading

## 8. Solved Numericals

### Example 1
**Q:** A gas occupies 5 L at 1 atm. What volume at 2 atm (T constant)?
```
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
1 × 5 = 2 × V₂
V₂ = 2.5 L
```

### Example 2
**Q:** A gas is at 300 K and 100 kPa. Find pressure when heated to 600 K (V constant).
```
P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂
100/300 = P₂/600
P₂ = 200 kPa
```
