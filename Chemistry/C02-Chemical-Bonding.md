# C02 — Chemical Bonding 🔥 Priority 1

> **Exam Importance:** Chemical bonding — types of bonds, their properties, and which molecules have which type of bond — generates 2–3 questions per paper. Ionic vs covalent vs metallic vs hydrogen bond distinctions are directly tested.

**Cross-links:** [C01 Periodic Table](C01-Periodic-Table-Properties.md) · [C07 Structure of Atom](C07-Structure-of-Atom.md) · [C08 States of Matter](C08-States-of-Matter-Gases.md)

## 1. Why Atoms Form Bonds

- Atoms form bonds to achieve **stable electronic configuration** (usually 8 electrons in outermost shell — **Octet rule**, except H and He which follow duet rule with 2)
- Bonding reduces potential energy → more stable state

---

## 2. Types of Chemical Bonds

| Bond Type | How Formed | Between | Properties | Example |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Ionic (Electrovalent)** | Transfer of electrons | Metal + Non-metal (large EN difference > 1.7) | High MP/BP; conducts when molten/dissolved; brittle; formed as ions | NaCl, MgO, CaCl₂, KBr |
| **Covalent** | Sharing of electron pairs | Non-metal + Non-metal (small EN difference < 1.7) | Low MP/BP; usually non-conductor; soft; discrete molecules | H₂O, CO₂, NH₃, CH₄, HCl |
| **Metallic** | Delocalised "sea" of electrons | Metal atoms | Good conductor; malleable; ductile; lustrous; high tensile strength | Fe, Cu, Na, Al |
| **Hydrogen bond** | H bonded to F, O, or N attracts adjacent electronegative atom | Molecules containing H-F, H-O, H-N | Weak but very important; responsible for high BP of water; determines protein structure | H₂O, HF, NH₃, DNA base pairs |
| **van der Waals forces** | Temporary induced dipole | All molecules | Very weak; increases with molecular size | Noble gases, nonpolar molecules |
| **Coordinate (Dative) bond** | Both electrons donated by one atom | One atom donates lone pair | Properties like covalent | [NH₄]⁺, H₃O⁺, BF₃·NH₃ |

## 3. Ionic Bonding — Deep Dive

**Formation Example — NaCl (Sodium Chloride):**
```
Na (2,8,1) → Na⁺ (2,8) + 1e⁻    [loses electron]
Cl (2,8,7) + 1e⁻ → Cl⁻ (2,8,8)  [gains electron]
Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl                 [ionic bond formed]
```

**Properties of Ionic Compounds:**
- Exist as **crystal lattice** (regular 3D arrangement of ions)
- **High melting and boiling points** (strong electrostatic attraction)
- **Conduct electricity** when molten or in aqueous solution (free ions), but NOT as solid
- **Soluble** in polar solvents (water); insoluble in non-polar solvents
- **Hard but brittle** (planes of ions can slide and repel)
- No individual molecules — giant ionic structure

---

## 4. Covalent Bonding — Deep Dive

**Types by number of shared pairs:**

| Bond | Shared Pairs | Example | Bond Strength |
|------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Single bond | 1 pair | H-H, C-H, H-Cl | Weakest |
| Double bond | 2 pairs | C=C, C=O, O=O | Stronger |
| Triple bond | 3 pairs | N≡N, C≡C, C≡N | Strongest |

**Examples of Covalent Molecules:**

| Molecule | Formula | Bond | Shape |
|---------|---------|------|-------|
| Water | H₂O | O-H (single, polar) | V-shaped/Bent |
| Carbon dioxide | CO₂ | C=O (double) | Linear |
| Ammonia | NH₃ | N-H (single) | Trigonal pyramidal |
| Methane | CH₄ | C-H (single) | Tetrahedral |
| Oxygen | O₂ | O=O (double) | — |
| Nitrogen | N₂ | N≡N (triple) | — |
| Ethylene | C₂H₄ | C=C (double) | Planar |
| Acetylene | C₂H₂ | C≡C (triple) | Linear |

**Properties of Covalent Compounds:**
- Low melting and boiling points (weak intermolecular forces)
- Usually do NOT conduct electricity (no free ions)
- Soluble in non-polar solvents (like dissolves like)
- Exist as discrete molecules

---

## 5. Polarity of Covalent Bonds

| Polarity | Condition | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Non-polar covalent | Equal sharing (same atoms) | H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂ |
| Polar covalent | Unequal sharing (different EN) | H₂O, HCl, NH₃ |

**Polar molecules** have permanent dipole moments → stronger intermolecular forces → higher BP.
**Water is highly polar** → good solvent for polar/ionic substances.

---

## 6. Hydrogen Bonding — High Importance

**Formation Conditions:**
1. Hydrogen must be bonded to a highly electronegative atom (F, O, or N)
2. Another electronegative atom (F, O, or N) nearby attracts the H

**Types:**

| Type | Occurs in | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| Intermolecular H-bond | Between different molecules | H₂O (water), HF, NH₃ |
| Intramolecular H-bond | Within the same molecule | o-nitrophenol, salicylaldehyde |

**Effects of Hydrogen Bonding:**

| Property affected | Effect | Example |
|-----------------|--------|---------|
| Boiling point | Increases significantly | H₂O boils at 100°C (without H-bond would be ~−80°C) |
| Solubility | Increases in water | Sugar dissolves in water (H-bonds with water) |
| Viscosity | Increases | Water more viscous than expected |
| Density of ice | Decreases (open structure) | Ice less dense than water — floats |
| DNA structure | Stabilises double helix | A-T and G-C base pairing by H-bonds |
| Protein structure | Stabilises α-helix and β-sheet | Secondary structure of proteins |

---

## 7. Metallic Bonding

**Model:** Metal atoms release valence electrons → form a "sea" of delocalised electrons surrounding positive metal ions (cations).

```
Metal → Metal cation + Delocalised electrons (electron sea)
```

**Properties explained by metallic bonding:**
- **Electrical conductivity:** Free electrons carry charge
- **Thermal conductivity:** Free electrons transfer heat
- **Lustre:** Electrons absorb and re-emit photons
- **Malleability/Ductility:** Ion layers slide without breaking (unlike ionic crystals)
- **High melting point:** Strong bond (increases with more valence electrons)

---

## 8. VSEPR Theory (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion)

> Electron pairs around central atom repel each other and adopt positions to minimise repulsion.

| Molecule | Central atom electron pairs | Shape |
|---------|---------------------------|-------|
| CH₄ (methane) | 4 bonding pairs | Tetrahedral (109.5°) |
| NH₃ (ammonia) | 3 bonding + 1 lone pair | Trigonal pyramidal |
| H₂O (water) | 2 bonding + 2 lone pairs | V-shaped/Bent (104.5°) |
| CO₂ | 2 double bonds | Linear (180°) |
| BF₃ | 3 bonding pairs | Trigonal planar (120°) |
| PCl₅ | 5 bonding pairs | Trigonal bipyramidal |
| SF₆ | 6 bonding pairs | Octahedral (90°) |

## 9. Lewis Structures (Electron Dot Structures)

| Molecule | Lewis Structure Description |
|---------|---------------------------|
| H₂O | O with 2 H atoms; O has 2 lone pairs |
| CO₂ | O=C=O; C has 2 double bonds |
| NH₃ | N with 3 H atoms; N has 1 lone pair |
| CH₄ | C with 4 H atoms; no lone pairs |
| N₂ | N≡N; each N has 1 lone pair |

|--------------|---------|-----------|---------|
| sp | Linear | 180° | BeF₂, CO₂, acetylene (C₂H₂) |
| sp² | Trigonal planar | 120° | BF₃, ethylene (C₂H₄), graphite |
| sp³ | Tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄, H₂O, NH₃, diamond |

## 11. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** Ionic compounds conduct electricity when **molten or in solution** (free ions) — NOT in solid state (ions are fixed in lattice).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** Covalent compounds generally do **NOT conduct electricity** (no ions or free electrons) — EXCEPT graphite (delocalised electrons between layers).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** Hydrogen bonding occurs only with F, O, N — NOT with carbon or other elements.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** Hydrogen bond is an **intermolecular force**, NOT a true chemical bond. It is much weaker than covalent or ionic bonds.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** Water's high boiling point, high specific heat, and surface tension — ALL due to hydrogen bonding.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** Metallic bonds are NOT individual bonds between two atoms — they involve a whole lattice of ions sharing an electron sea.
