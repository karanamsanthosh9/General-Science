## 1. History of the Periodic Table

| Scientist | Contribution | Year |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| **Döbereiner** | Law of Triads — elements in groups of 3 with middle element having avg properties | 1829 |
| **Newlands** | Law of Octaves — every 8th element has similar properties | 1865 |
| **Mendeleev** | First periodic table arranged by atomic mass; left gaps for undiscovered elements | 1869 |
| **Moseley** | Arranged by atomic number (Modern Periodic Law) | 1913 |
| **Modern Periodic Table** | 118 elements; 7 periods; 18 groups | Present |

**Modern Periodic Law:** *Properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.*

## 2. Structure of the Periodic Table

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Total elements confirmed | 118 |
| Periods (horizontal rows) | 7 |
| Groups (vertical columns) | 18 |
| Metals | ~90 (approximately 80% of elements) |
| Non-metals | 22 |
| Metalloids (semimetals) | 7 (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po) |
| Transition metals | Groups 3–12 (d-block) |
| Lanthanides | Period 6, f-block (Ce to Lu) |
| Actinides | Period 7, f-block (Th to Lr) — mostly radioactive |

### 2.1 Periods

| Period | Elements | Shells filled |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| Period 1 | H, He (2 elements) | K shell |
| Period 2 | Li to Ne (8 elements) | L shell |
| Period 3 | Na to Ar (8 elements) | M shell |
| Period 4 | K to Kr (18 elements) | M-N shells |
| Period 5 | Rb to Xe (18 elements) | N-O shells |
| Period 6 | Cs to Rn (32 elements) | includes lanthanides |
| Period 7 | Fr to Og (32 elements) | includes actinides (mostly synthetic/radioactive) |

## 4. Periodic Trends — Master Comparison Table

| Property | Across Period (Left → Right) | Down a Group (Top → Bottom) | Reason |
|---------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|--------|
| **Atomic radius** | **Decreases** ↓ | **Increases** ↑ | Across: more protons, same shells (electrons pulled in); Down: more shells added |
| **Ionic radius** | Cations smaller; anions larger than atoms | Increases ↑ | More shells down the group |
| **Ionisation energy (IE)** | **Increases** ↑ | **Decreases** ↓ | Across: more nuclear charge; Down: outer electrons farther, shielded |
| **Electron affinity** | Increases ↑ (generally) | Decreases ↓ | Similar to IE |
| **Electronegativity** | **Increases** ↑ | **Decreases** ↓ | Highest = F (top-right); lowest = Cs (bottom-left) |
| **Metallic character** | **Decreases** ↓ | **Increases** ↑ | More metallic toward bottom-left of PT |
| **Non-metallic character** | **Increases** ↑ | **Decreases** ↓ | More non-metallic toward top-right |
| **Reactivity of metals** | Decreases ↓ | **Increases** ↑ | Down group = more reactive metals |
| **Reactivity of non-metals** | Increases ↑ | **Decreases** ↓ | Up group = more reactive non-metals |

> **EXAM CRITICAL:** Atomic radius DECREASES across a period — this is counter-intuitive but correct.

## 9. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** Most abundant element in Earth's **atmosphere** = Nitrogen (N₂, 78%), NOT oxygen.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** Most abundant element in Earth's **crust** = Oxygen (~46%), NOT silicon or aluminium.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** Most abundant **metal** in crust = Aluminium, NOT iron.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** Fluorine is most electronegative — NOT oxygen. Order: F > O > Cl > N.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** Atomic radius DECREASES across a period (more protons pulling electrons in) — many students incorrectly say it increases.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** Noble gases are generally inert but xenon (Xe) and krypton (Kr) CAN form compounds (XeF₂, XeF₄, KrF₂) — "inert" is not absolute.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 7:** Bromine (Br) is a LIQUID at room temperature but it is a NON-METAL. Mercury (Hg) is liquid but it is a METAL.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 8:** Hydrogen is placed in Group 1 but it is NOT an alkali metal — it is a non-metal.

## 10. Frequently Confused Concepts

| Concept A | Concept B | Distinction |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Atomic number | Mass number | Z = protons only; A = protons + neutrons |
| Period | Group | Period = horizontal row; Group = vertical column |
| Ionisation energy | Electron affinity | IE = energy to remove electron; EA = energy released when electron added |
| Electronegativity | Electron affinity | EN = tendency to attract bonding electrons; EA = energy change when electron added to isolated atom |
| Alkali metals | Alkaline earth metals | Group 1 vs Group 2; alkali more reactive |
| Lanthanides | Actinides | Both f-block; lanthanides period 6; actinides period 7 (mostly radioactive) |
