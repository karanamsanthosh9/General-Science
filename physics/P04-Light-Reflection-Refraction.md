## 3. Nature of Light

| Property | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Nature | Electromagnetic wave (transverse wave) |
| Speed in vacuum | 3 × 10⁸ m/s (c) |
| Speed in medium | Slows down (v < c) |
| Wavelength range | ~400 nm (violet) to ~700 nm (red) |
| Wave-particle duality | Acts as wave (interference, diffraction) and particle (photoelectric effect) |
| Does not need medium | Can travel in vacuum |

## 4. Reflection of Light

### 4.1 Laws of Reflection

1. The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal at the point of incidence all lie in the **same plane**
2. The **angle of incidence = angle of reflection** (∠i = ∠r)

### 4.2 Types of Reflection

| Type | Surface | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| Regular (specular) reflection | Smooth, polished | Clear image (mirror) |
| Irregular (diffuse) reflection | Rough | No clear image (wall, paper) |

### 4.3 Mirror Formula

```
1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Magnification: m = −v/u = h'/h
```

**Sign Convention (New Cartesian):**
- All distances measured from the **pole** of the mirror
- Distances in the direction of incident light = **positive** (+)
- Distances against the direction of incident light = **negative** (−)
- Heights above principal axis = **positive**; below = **negative**

| Quantity | Concave Mirror | Convex Mirror |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| Focal length (f) | Negative (−f) | Positive (+f) |
| Centre of curvature | In front | Behind mirror |
| Nature of image | Real (mostly) or Virtual | Always Virtual |

## 5. Spherical Mirrors

### 5.1 Concave Mirror (Converging Mirror)

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature of Image | Size | Use |
|----------------|---------------|----------------|------|-----|
| At infinity | At F | Real, inverted | Point | Searchlights, headlights |
| Beyond C | Between F and C | Real, inverted | Diminished | — |
| At C | At C | Real, inverted | Same size | — |
| Between F and C | Beyond C | Real, inverted | Magnified | — |
| At F | At infinity | Real, inverted | Very large | — |
| Between F and P (pole) | Behind mirror | Virtual, erect | Magnified | Shaving mirror, dentist mirror, solar furnace |

**Uses of Concave Mirror:**
- Shaving/makeup mirror (magnified erect image)
- Dentist's examination mirror
- Reflector in torchlight, headlights, solar furnace, searchlight
- Ophthalmoscope (eye doctor)

### 5.2 Convex Mirror (Diverging Mirror)

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature | Size |
|----------------|---------------|--------|------|
| **Anywhere** | **Between P and F (behind mirror)** | **Virtual, erect** | **Diminished (always)** |

> **Key Fact:** Convex mirror always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image — regardless of object position.

**Uses of Convex Mirror:**
- **Rear-view mirrors** in vehicles (wider field of view)
- CCTV cameras (wide angle)
- Security mirrors in shops and ATMs
- Rear-view mirrors on roads/highways (sharp bends)

**Why convex mirror in rear-view?**
- Gives erect image → driver can see correctly
- Gives diminished image → wider field of view (more area visible)
- Image appears to be farther than actual → driver must be aware of this

### 5.3 Mirror Comparison Table

| Feature | Concave | Convex | Plane |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| Shape | Inward curved | Outward curved | Flat |
| Nature | Converging | Diverging | — |
| Focal length | Negative | Positive | Infinity |
| Image | Real (mostly) | Always virtual | Virtual |
| Use | Shaving, torch, dental, solar furnace | Rear-view, CCTV | Dressing mirrors |

## 6. Refraction of Light

### 6.1 Definition
When light travels from one medium to another (of different optical density), it changes its speed and bends at the interface — this is **refraction**.

### 6.2 Laws of Refraction

1. The incident ray, refracted ray, and normal at the point of incidence lie in the **same plane**
2. **Snell's Law:** n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂

### 6.3 Refractive Index

```
Refractive index (n) = Speed of light in vacuum / Speed in medium
n = c / v
n = sin i / sin r    (Snell's law)
```

**Refractive Index Values — PYQ Table:**

| Medium | Refractive Index |
|--------|----------------|
| Vacuum / Air | 1.0 (reference) |
| Water | 1.33 |
| Glass (crown) | 1.5 |
| Diamond | **2.42** (very high) |
| Ice | 1.31 |
| Alcohol | 1.36 |
| Quartz | 1.46 |

> **Higher refractive index = slower light = denser medium optically**

### 6.4 Lens Formula

```
1/f = 1/v − 1/u

Magnification: m = v/u = h'/h

Power of lens: P = 1/f (f in metres)
Unit of power: Dioptre (D)
```

**Sign convention for lens:**
- Object is always on left; incident light goes left to right
- Distances measured from **optical centre**
- Right of optical centre = positive; Left = negative

## 7. Lenses

### 7.1 Convex (Converging) Lens

| Object Position | Image | Nature | Use |
|----------------|-------|--------|-----|
| At infinity | At F₂ | Real, inverted, point | Camera, telescope |
| Beyond 2F₁ | Between F₂ and 2F₂ | Real, inverted, diminished | Camera, human eye |
| At 2F₁ | At 2F₂ | Real, inverted, same size | — |
| Between F₁ and 2F₁ | Beyond 2F₂ | Real, inverted, magnified | Projector |
| At F₁ | At infinity | Real, inverted, very large | Searchlight |
| Between O and F₁ | Same side as object | Virtual, erect, magnified | Magnifying glass, reading glass |

**Uses of Convex Lens:**
- Magnifying glass
- Camera, projector
- Microscope (objective + eyepiece)
- Refracting telescope
- Correction of **Hypermetropia (long sight)**

### 7.2 Concave (Diverging) Lens

| Object Position | Image | Nature |
|----------------|-------|--------|
| **Anywhere** | **Between O and F₁** | **Virtual, erect, diminished (always)** |

**Uses of Concave Lens:**
- Correction of **Myopia (short sight)**
- Peepholes in doors (wide-angle view)
- Galilean telescope (eyepiece)

### 7.3 Power of Lens

```
P = 1/f     (f in metres, P in Dioptre)
Converging lens: f = positive → P = positive
Diverging lens: f = negative → P = negative

For combination of lenses:
P_total = P₁ + P₂ + P₃ + ...
```

## 8. Eye Defects and Corrections

### 8.1 Structure of the Eye (Quick Facts)

| Part | Function |
|------|---------|
| Cornea | Refracts light (most of it) |
| Iris | Controls amount of light entering |
| Pupil | Opening in iris |
| Lens | Fine-focuses light on retina |
| Ciliary muscles | Change shape of lens (accommodation) |
| Retina | Screen — contains rods and cones |
| Rods | Detect brightness (dim light/night vision) |
| Cones | Detect colour (bright light/colour vision) |
| Optic nerve | Transmits signals to brain |
| Blind spot | Point where optic nerve exits — no vision here |

### 8.2 Eye Defects Table

| Defect | Problem | Cause | Correction | Lens Used | PYQ |
|--------|---------|-------|-----------|----------|-----|
| **Myopia** (short-sightedness / near-sightedness) | Cannot see distant objects clearly | Eyeball too long OR lens too thick | Concave (diverging) lens | Negative power (−D) | 🔥 |
| **Hypermetropia** (long-sightedness / far-sightedness) | Cannot see near objects clearly | Eyeball too short OR lens too thin | Convex (converging) lens | Positive power (+D) | 🔥 |
| **Presbyopia** | Cannot see near OR far clearly (age-related) | Weakening of ciliary muscles (age) | Bifocal lens (both concave + convex) | Bifocal | 🔥 |
| **Astigmatism** | Objects appear blurred/distorted in some orientations | Irregular curvature of cornea | Cylindrical lens | Cylindrical | ⭐ |
| **Colour blindness** | Cannot distinguish certain colours (commonly red-green) | Absence/defect of colour-sensitive cones | No optical correction | — | ⭐ |
| **Cataract** | Lens becomes cloudy/opaque | Ageing, UV exposure, diabetes | Surgical replacement of lens | — | ⭐ |

## 9. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

### 9.1 Conditions for TIR

1. Light must travel from **denser medium to rarer medium** (higher n → lower n)
2. **Angle of incidence must exceed the Critical Angle (θc)**

```
sin θc = 1/n    (relative to vacuum/air)
or: n₁ sin θc = n₂ × 1 (when n₂ = 1 for air)
```

| Medium | Critical Angle |
|--------|---------------|
| Glass | ~42° |
| Water | ~48.8° |
| Diamond | ~24.4° |

> Diamond has the SMALLEST critical angle → most TIR → maximum brilliance and sparkle!

### 9.2 Applications of TIR

| Application | How TIR is used | PYQ |
|------------|----------------|-----|
| **Optical fibre** | Light travels through glass/plastic fibre by repeated TIR; used in telecom, endoscopy | 🔥 |
| **Diamond sparkle** | Very small critical angle (24.4°) → light undergoes multiple TIR → exits brilliantly | 🔥 |
| **Mirage** | Hot air near ground (less dense) → light bends → appears like water reflection on road | 🔥 |
| **Periscope / Binoculars** | Glass prisms use TIR to redirect light | ⭐ |
| **Looming** | Object appears to float above horizon in cold regions | 📘 |

## 10. Dispersion of Light

### 10.1 Definition
Splitting of white light into its constituent colours when passing through a prism — because different wavelengths refract by different amounts.

```
VIBGYOR: Violet → Indigo → Blue → Green → Yellow → Orange → Red
```

| Colour | Wavelength | Bending in Prism | Speed in Glass |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Violet | ~400 nm (shortest) | Most (highest n) | Slowest |
| Red | ~700 nm (longest) | Least (lowest n) | Fastest |

> **Mnemonic:** "**V**ery **I**ntense **B**attles **G**ive **Y**ellow **O**range **R**edness" = VIBGYOR

### 10.2 Rainbow

- Formed when sunlight is dispersed by water droplets in the atmosphere
- Involves: **refraction + internal reflection + refraction** in water droplets
- **Primary rainbow:** Red on outside, violet on inside
- **Secondary rainbow:** Reverse colours (violet outside, red inside); dimmer
- Observer must have sun BEHIND them to see rainbow

### 10.3 Scattering of Light

**Rayleigh's Law of Scattering:**
```
Scattering intensity ∝ 1/λ⁴
(shorter wavelengths scatter more)
```

| Phenomenon | Colour | Reason |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Sky is blue | Blue light scattered most (short λ) | Blue scattered in all directions → sky appears blue |
| Sunset/sunrise appears red | Red travels through maximum atmosphere (long λ, least scattered) | When sun is at horizon, blue is scattered away, red remains |
| Sun appears white at noon | Light comes directly (no/less scattering) | All colours together = white |
| Clouds appear white | Water droplets scatter all wavelengths equally | All colours scattered equally |
| Danger signals are red | Red light scattered least (travels farthest) | Maximum range without scattering |

## 11. Other Optical Phenomena

### 11.1 Twinkling of Stars vs Planets

| Feature | Stars | Planets |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Appear to twinkle? | Yes | No |
| Reason | Stars are point sources; light bends continuously through varying atmospheric layers → appears to twinkle | Planets are extended sources (appear as disc); averaging cancels twinkling effect |

### 11.2 Mirages

- **Desert mirage (inferior mirage):** Hot air near ground → lower refractive index → light curves upward → appears as water (TIR)
- **Superior mirage:** Cold air near ground → occurs in polar regions; objects appear elevated

### 11.3 Optical Instruments

| Instrument | Type of Lens/Mirror | Use |
|-----------|-------------------|-----|
| Simple microscope | Convex lens | Magnified virtual image |
| Compound microscope | Convex (objective, short f) + Convex (eyepiece, long f) | Highly magnified |
| Astronomical telescope | Convex objective + Convex eyepiece | Distant celestial objects |
| Galilean telescope | Convex objective + Concave eyepiece | Terrestrial; erect image |
| Periscope | Two plane mirrors (or TIR prisms) | See over obstacles |
| Camera | Convex lens | Real, inverted, diminished image on film/sensor |

## 12. Solved Numericals

### Numerical 1
**Q:** A concave mirror has a focal length of 20 cm. An object is placed 30 cm in front. Find image distance.
```
Given: f = −20 cm (concave), u = −30 cm
1/f = 1/v + 1/u
1/(−20) = 1/v + 1/(−30)
1/v = −1/20 + 1/30 = −3/60 + 2/60 = −1/60
v = −60 cm
Image is 60 cm in front of mirror (real, inverted)
```

### Numerical 2
**Q:** An object is placed 40 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find image distance.
```
Given: f = +10 cm, u = −40 cm (lens formula uses opposite sign convention)
1/f = 1/v − 1/u
1/10 = 1/v − 1/(−40) = 1/v + 1/40
1/v = 1/10 − 1/40 = 4/40 − 1/40 = 3/40
v = 40/3 ≈ 13.3 cm (positive → real image on other side)
```

### Numerical 3
**Q:** A lens has focal length 25 cm. Find its power in Dioptre.
```
P = 1/f = 1/0.25 m = +4 D (converging lens)
```

### Numerical 4
**Q:** Light travels from water (n=1.33) to glass (n=1.5) with angle of incidence 30°. Find angle of refraction.
```
n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂
1.33 × sin 30° = 1.5 × sin θ₂
1.33 × 0.5 = 1.5 × sin θ₂
sin θ₂ = 0.665/1.5 = 0.443
θ₂ = sin⁻¹(0.443) ≈ 26.3°
```

## 13. Shortcut Tricks & Memory Techniques

> **"Concave = Cave-in"** → hollow side faces object → CONVERGES light → used in torches, shaving mirrors.

> **"Convex = BULGing out"** → always gives VIRTUAL, ERECT, DIMINISHED image → used in rear-view mirrors.

> **"My Ear Can Never See Anything Properly"** → Myopia-Ear(−negative lens/concave), Hypermetropia-Converging(+positive/convex), No Correction, Surgical — acronym for eye defects.

> **Simpler:** "**M → Minus (concave)**" = Myopia corrected by concave (−ve). "**H → Helpful convex (+ve)**" = Hypermetropia corrected by convex lens.

> **"VIBGYOR"** — in a prism, Violet bends MORE, Red bends LEAST. In a spectrum from prism: Red is at the TOP, Violet at the BOTTOM (Red goes straight, violet bends most downward).

> **"Diamond sparkles because it TRAPS light"** — lowest critical angle (24.4°) → maximum TIR.

> **"Blue sky = Rayleigh"** → Short λ scatters more → Blue scattered most → sky blue. Long λ (red) not scattered → red sunset.

## 14. Frequently Confused Concepts

| Concept A | Concept B | Key Difference |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|
| Concave mirror | Convex mirror | Concave = inward = converging (mostly real image); Convex = outward = diverging (always virtual) |
| Concave lens | Convex lens | Concave = diverging (corrects myopia); Convex = converging (corrects hypermetropia) |
| Myopia | Hypermetropia | Myopia = near objects clear, far blurred; Hypermetropia = far clear, near blurred |
| Real image | Virtual image | Real = light rays actually converge (can be captured on screen); Virtual = rays appear to diverge from a point |
| Dispersion | Scattering | Dispersion = white light splits in prism; Scattering = particles redirect light (blue sky) |
| TIR | Reflection | TIR = light cannot exit denser medium; Regular reflection = mirror/polished surface |
| Critical angle | Angle of incidence | Critical angle is the specific angle above which TIR occurs |
| Stars twinkle | Planets don't | Stars = point source → atmospheric refraction → twinkling; Planets = extended disc → averaging cancels |

## 15. Exam Traps

> ⚠️ **TRAP 1:** Speed of light is 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum — it SLOWS DOWN in any medium (even air, slightly).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 2:** Refractive index of diamond is ~2.42 — very HIGH — that's why it has a small critical angle and sparkles so much.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 3:** Red light has the LONGEST wavelength (~700 nm) and bends LEAST in a prism. Violet bends MOST (shortest λ).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 4:** Planets do NOT twinkle (they are extended sources, not point sources).

> ⚠️ **TRAP 5:** Convex mirror ALWAYS gives virtual, erect, diminished image — regardless of object position.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 6:** Concave lens ALWAYS gives virtual, erect, diminished image — regardless of object position.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 7:** Power of converging lens = POSITIVE dioptre; diverging lens = NEGATIVE dioptre.

> ⚠️ **TRAP 8:** In rear-view mirrors (convex), objects appear farther than they actually are — this is why traffic rules say "objects in mirror are closer than they appear".

> ⚠️ **TRAP 9:** Light bends TOWARD normal when entering denser medium; AWAY from normal in rarer medium.

## 16. Must-Remember Points

- Speed of light in vacuum = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
- Laws of reflection: ∠i = ∠r; incident ray, reflected ray, normal — same plane
- Concave mirror: converging; mostly real images; uses = shaving, headlight, solar furnace
- Convex mirror: diverging; always virtual+erect+diminished; use = rear-view mirror
- Refractive index n = c/v; higher n = denser medium optically
- TIR: denser → rarer medium, angle > critical angle
- Diamond: n = 2.42, critical angle = 24.4° → maximum TIR → brilliance
- Optical fibre: uses TIR → data transmission, endoscopy
- VIBGYOR: Violet bends most (short λ); Red bends least (long λ)
- Myopia → Concave lens (−D); Hypermetropia → Convex lens (+D)
- Blue sky: Rayleigh scattering (short λ scattered more)
- Red sunset: long λ (red) travels through more atmosphere without scattering
