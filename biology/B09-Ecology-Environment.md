## 1. Ecological Levels of Organisation

```
Organism (individual) → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere
```

| Level | Definition | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Organism | Single living individual | One tiger |
| Population | All individuals of same species in an area | All tigers in Ranthambore |
| Community | All populations of different species in an area | All plants, animals, microbes in Ranthambore |
| Ecosystem | Community + its abiotic environment (soil, water, climate) | Ranthambore forest ecosystem |
| Biome | Large geographical area with similar climate and vegetation | Tropical rainforest, desert, tundra |
| Biosphere | All ecosystems on Earth combined = zone of life | Earth's biosphere |

## 3. Food Chain and Food Web

**Food chain:** Linear sequence showing flow of energy and matter from producers to consumers.

```
Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk
(Producer) (1° Consumer) (2° Consumer) (3° Consumer) (4° Consumer)
```

| Trophic Level | Name | Examples |
|--------------|------|---------|
| 1st | **Producers (Autotrophs)** | Green plants, phytoplankton, cyanobacteria |
| 2nd | **Primary consumers (Herbivores)** | Grasshopper, rabbit, deer, cow |
| 3rd | **Secondary consumers (Carnivores)** | Frog, small fish |
| 4th | **Tertiary consumers** | Snake, large fish |
| 5th+ | **Top predators** | Hawk, eagle, tiger, shark |

**Decomposers (Saprotrophs):** Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter → return nutrients to soil; NOT part of main food chain (separate detritus food chain).

**Food web:** Multiple interconnected food chains in an ecosystem.

---

## 4. Energy Flow — 10% Rule (Lindeman's Efficiency)

> **"Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next."**
> The remaining ~90% is lost as heat (respiration, movement, etc.)

```
1000 J at producer → 100 J at 1° consumer → 10 J at 2° consumer → 1 J at 3° consumer

Energy lost at each level ≈ 90%
Energy transferred to next level ≈ 10%
```

**Implication:** Eating lower on the food chain is more energy-efficient. Vegetarian diet supports more people from same land than meat-based diet.

## 5. Ecological Pyramids

| Pyramid | What it shows | Always upright? | Exception |
|---------|--------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Pyramid of numbers** | Number of organisms at each trophic level | No | Tree-caterpillar-bird (inverted — one tree supports many caterpillars) |
| **Pyramid of biomass** | Total mass (dry weight) at each trophic level | Usually upright | Aquatic ecosystem (inverted) |
| **Pyramid of energy** | Energy at each trophic level | ALWAYS UPRIGHT | No exception |

> **Pyramid of energy is ALWAYS upright** — this is a direct exam question.

## 6. Biogeochemical Cycles

### 6.1 Carbon Cycle

```
CO₂ in atmosphere ↔ Plants (photosynthesis fixes CO₂ → organic C)
                  ↔ Animals (eat plants; respiration releases CO₂)
                  ↔ Decomposers (break down dead matter → CO₂)
                  ↔ Fossil fuels (ancient organic matter; burning releases CO₂)
```

### 6.2 Nitrogen Cycle

```
N₂ in atmosphere (78%)
→ Nitrogen fixation: Rhizobium (legumes), Azotobacter, lightning → NH₃/NH₄⁺
→ Nitrification: Nitrosomonas (NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻); Nitrobacter (NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻)
→ Assimilation: Plants absorb NO₃⁻ → proteins; animals eat plants
→ Ammonification: Dead organisms → NH₃/NH₄⁺ by decomposers
→ Denitrification: Pseudomonas (NO₃⁻ → N₂ back to atmosphere)
```

**Key nitrogen-fixing organisms:**
- **Rhizobium** — in root nodules of legumes (symbiotic)
- **Azotobacter** — free-living in soil
- **Cyanobacteria** (Blue-green algae) — free-living in water/soil

## 7. Types of Ecosystems

| Ecosystem | Key Features | Examples |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Forest** | High biodiversity; dense canopy | Tropical rainforest (Amazon, Western Ghats), temperate forest |
| **Grassland** | Open grassy plains; few trees | Savanna (Africa), Prairies (USA), Deccan plateau |
| **Desert** | Very low rainfall; extreme temperatures | Sahara, Thar, Atacama |
| **Marine** | Salt water; largest ecosystem | Oceans, seas, coral reefs |
| **Freshwater** | Fresh water; rivers, lakes, ponds | Ganges, Amazon river |
| **Wetlands** | Flooded/waterlogged; high productivity | Mangroves, swamps, marshes |
| **Estuaries** | Where river meets sea; mix of fresh + salt | River deltas |
| **Coral reef** | Extremely high biodiversity; "rainforest of the sea" | Great Barrier Reef, Lakshadweep |
| **Tundra** | Very cold; permafrost; low biodiversity | Arctic, Antarctic margins |

## 8. Biodiversity

**Definition:** Variety of life at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels.

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Genetic diversity | Variation within a species (different alleles) |
| Species diversity | Number of different species in an area |
| Ecosystem diversity | Variety of ecosystems |

**Hotspots (Biodiversity Hotspots):**
- Regions with exceptional biodiversity AND high threat
- **India's biodiversity hotspots:** Western Ghats (+ Sri Lanka), Himalaya, Indo-Burma region, Sundaland (extends to NE India)

**India's biodiversity status:**
- 12 out of 17 megadiverse countries
- ~8% of world's biodiversity on ~2.4% of land area
- 45,000+ species of plants; 91,000+ species of animals

## 9. Environmental Issues

### 9.1 Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming

| Gas | Main Source | Global Warming Potential (GWP) |
|----|------------|-------------------------------|
| CO₂ | Fossil fuels, deforestation | 1 (reference) |
| CH₄ | Cattle, rice paddies, landfills | 25 |
| N₂O | Fertilisers | 298 |
| CFCs | Refrigerants (now banned) | 4,000–14,000 |
| Water vapour | Evaporation | Largest volume but natural |

**Effects of global warming:** Sea level rise; more extreme weather; melting glaciers; shifting seasons; coral bleaching; species extinction.

### 9.2 Ozone Depletion

- **Ozone layer:** Stratosphere 15–35 km; blocks UV-B and UV-C
- **Cause of depletion:** CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) from old refrigerants, aerosols
- **Effect of depletion:** Increased UV → skin cancer, cataracts, immune suppression, crop damage
- **Ozone hole:** Largest over Antarctica
- **Solution:** **Montreal Protocol (1987)** — phase out CFCs; most successful international environmental agreement

### 9.3 Acid Rain

- pH < 5.6
- Caused by SO₂ and NOₓ dissolving in rain
- Effects: Damages forests, acidifies lakes, corrodes buildings (Taj Mahal)

## 10. Conservation

| Conservation Type | Description | Examples |
|-----------------|-------------|---------|
| **In-situ (on-site)** | Conserve species in natural habitat | National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves |
| **Ex-situ (off-site)** | Conserve outside natural habitat | Zoos, botanical gardens, gene banks, seed banks |

**India's protected areas:**
- **National parks:** Strict protection; no grazing/human interference
- **Wildlife sanctuaries:** Moderate protection; some human use allowed
- **Tiger reserves:** Special protection under Project Tiger (started 1973)
- **Biosphere reserves:** Include core, buffer, and transition zones

**Famous wildlife protected areas (India):**
- Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand) — first national park in India (1936)
- Kaziranga National Park (Assam) — One-horned rhinoceros
- Sundarbans (West Bengal/Bangladesh) — Mangroves; Bengal tiger
- Gir National Park (Gujarat) — Asiatic lion (ONLY place)

## 11. Ecological Relationships

| Relationship | Species A | Species B | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Predation** | Benefits (+) | Harmed (−) | Lion-deer |
| **Parasitism** | Benefits (+) | Harmed (−) | Tapeworm-human |
| **Competition** | Harmed (−) | Harmed (−) | Two species for same food |
| **Mutualism** | Benefits (+) | Benefits (+) | Bee-flower, Rhizobium-legume, clownfish-anemone |
| **Commensalism** | Benefits (+) | Neutral (0) | Egret-cattle, orchid on tree |
| **Amensalism** | Neutral (0) | Harmed (−) | Penicillin vs bacteria, black walnut tree vs other plants |
