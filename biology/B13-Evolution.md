## 1. Key Evolutionary Concepts

### 1.1 Darwin's Theory of Natural Selection

**Charles Darwin (1809–1882):** Published "On the Origin of Species" (1859).

**Key Points:**
1. **Variation:** Individuals in a population show natural variations
2. **Overproduction:** More offspring are produced than can survive (Malthus's idea applied)
3. **Struggle for existence:** Competition for resources (food, space, mates)
4. **Survival of the fittest:** Individuals best adapted to environment survive and reproduce
5. **Natural selection:** Environment "selects" the best-adapted individuals
6. **Inheritance:** Favourable traits are passed to offspring
7. Over time → new species (speciation)

**Alfred Russel Wallace** independently arrived at similar theory; both presented jointly to Linnean Society (1858).

## 2. Lamarck's Theory (Pre-Darwinian)

**Jean-Baptiste Lamarck (1744–1829):**
- Theory of **use and disuse** of organs
- Theory of **inheritance of acquired characteristics**
- **Example:** Giraffes stretched necks to reach leaves → longer neck passed to offspring
- **Disproved:** Acquired characteristics are NOT inherited (Weismann's experiment)

## 3. Evidence for Evolution

| Evidence | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Fossil record** | Preserved remains of ancient organisms; show progressive complexity |
| **Comparative anatomy** | Homologous organs (same origin, different function) indicate common ancestry |
| **Analogous organs** | Similar function, different origin → convergent evolution |
| **Vestigial organs** | Reduced, non-functional remnants (e.g., appendix, coccyx, wisdom teeth in humans) |
| **Embryology** | Embryos of different vertebrates look similar at early stages (Haeckel's biogenetic law) |
| **Molecular biology** | DNA similarity between species indicates evolutionary relationships; cytochrome c protein |
| **Biogeography** | Species distribution on Earth reflects evolutionary history |

---

## 4. Homologous vs Analogous Organs

| Type | Definition | Example | Implication |
|------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Homologous organs** | Same origin/structure, different function | Forelimb of human, bat wing, whale flipper, cat paw | Common ancestry (divergent evolution) |
| **Analogous organs** | Different origin, same function | Bat wing (skin fold) vs bird wing (feathers); eye of vertebrate vs eye of octopus | Convergent evolution (no common ancestry; similar problems → similar solutions) |

---

## 5. Key Evolutionary Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Speciation** | Formation of new species; usually due to reproductive isolation |
| **Allopatric speciation** | Speciation due to geographical isolation (mountains, rivers) |
| **Sympatric speciation** | Speciation without geographical separation |
| **Gene pool** | All genes in a population |
| **Genetic drift** | Random changes in allele frequency (important in small populations) |
| **Founder effect** | Small group colonises new area → limited genetic diversity |
| **Bottleneck effect** | Population drastically reduced → low genetic diversity |
| **Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium** | Allele frequencies constant in absence of evolution |

## 6. Human Evolution

| Species | Period (approx.) | Key Feature |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| *Australopithecus* | 4–2 MYA | Upright posture; small brain (~400 cc) |
| *Homo habilis* | 2.5–1.5 MYA | "Handy man"; first tool use; brain ~700 cc |
| *Homo erectus* | 1.8–0.3 MYA | Walked fully upright; fire use; brain ~900 cc |
| *Homo heidelbergensis* | 0.7–0.2 MYA | — |
| *Homo neanderthalensis* | 0.4–0.04 MYA | Ice-age Europe; buried dead; brain ~1400 cc |
| *Homo sapiens* | 0.3 MYA – present | Modern humans; brain ~1400 cc; complex culture |
