# B12 — Plant Kingdom Classification 📘 Priority 3

> **Exam Importance:** Major divisions of plant kingdom, distinguishing features, and examples appear in SSC exams at moderate frequency. Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms are the key categories.

---

## 1. Plant Kingdom (Kingdom Plantae)

**Key features:** Eukaryotic, multicellular, photosynthetic (autotrophic), cell wall of cellulose.

---

## 2. Classification — Whittaker's Five Kingdom System (Modern)

| Kingdom | Type | Examples |
|---------|------|---------|
| Monera | Prokaryote | Bacteria, Cyanobacteria |
| Protista | Unicellular eukaryote | Amoeba, Paramecium, Algae |
| Fungi | Eukaryote, heterotroph, chitin cell wall | Mushroom, Yeast, Mould |
| Plantae | Multicellular, photosynthetic | All plants |
| Animalia | Multicellular, heterotroph, no cell wall | All animals |

---

## 3. Plant Kingdom Divisions

### 3.1 Thallophyta (Algae)

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Body | Thallus (undifferentiated); no roots, stems, leaves |
| Reproduction | Asexual and sexual |
| Habitat | Mostly aquatic (marine and freshwater) |
| Examples | *Spirogyra*, *Ulva* (sea lettuce), *Fucus*, *Chara*, *Volvox* |

**Economic importance:**
- Diatomite (from diatoms) → polishing, insulation
- Agar (from red algae *Gelidium*) → culture medium
- Carrageenan (red algae) → food thickener
- Alginates (brown algae) → food, pharmaceuticals
- Spirulina → high-protein food supplement

### 3.2 Bryophyta (Mosses and Liverworts)

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Body | Flat thallus or leafy; no true roots (rhizoids); no vascular tissue |
| Common name | "Amphibians of plant kingdom" (need water for reproduction) |
| Reproduction | Need water for fertilisation (flagellated sperm) |
| Examples | Mosses (*Funaria*, *Sphagnum*), Liverworts (*Marchantia*), Hornworts |

**Sphagnum (peat moss):**
- Forms peat; important fuel source in some countries
- Absorbs large amounts of water; used in gardening

### 3.3 Pteridophyta (Ferns and Horsetails)

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Body | True roots, stems, leaves; first vascular land plants |
| Vascular tissue | Present (xylem + phloem) — first among plants |
| Reproduction | Spores; still need water for fertilisation |
| Examples | Ferns (*Dryopteris*, *Pteris*), Horsetails (*Equisetum*), Club mosses (*Selaginella*) |

### 3.4 Gymnosperms (Naked seed plants)

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Seeds | Present; NOT enclosed in fruit ("naked seeds") |
| Flowers | Absent; instead have cones (strobili) |
| Examples | Pine (*Pinus*), Deodar, Cycas, Ginkgo, Fir, Spruce, *Agathis* |
| Significance | Pine — timber, resin; Cycas — sago (cycad starch); Taxus — Taxol (anti-cancer drug) |

### 3.5 Angiosperms (Flowering plants — covered seeds)

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Seeds | Enclosed within fruit |
| Flowers | Present (attract pollinators) |
| Fruit | Develops from ovary after fertilisation |
| Largest division | Most species of plants |
| Divided into | Monocots and Dicots |

---

## 4. Monocots vs Dicots

| Feature | Monocots (Monocotyledons) | Dicots (Dicotyledons) |
|---------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Cotyledons in seed | 1 | 2 |
| Leaf veins | Parallel venation | Reticulate (net-like) venation |
| Roots | Fibrous roots | Taproot |
| Stem vascular bundles | Scattered | Arranged in a ring |
| Flower parts | In multiples of 3 | In multiples of 4 or 5 |
| Examples | Rice, wheat, maize, sugarcane, onion, banana, grass, lily | Mango, pea, rose, sunflower, mustard, potato, tomato |
